\telosmasthead[Pine Lake \& North Lake --- the whole year on one sheet]

Fish do not follow the calendar; they follow water temperature, and the calendar
is only a rough proxy for it. A cold spring pushes everything two weeks late. Use
the temperature column, not the month, whenever you can measure it.

\section{The year by water temperature}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.115\linewidth}p{0.115\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Water} & \textbf{Roughly} & \textbf{What is happening, and what to do}\\
\midrule
32--40\,\unitof{^\circ F} & Dec--Mar & Ice. Panfish in the bays, perch on the flats, pike on tip-ups over weed edges. Everything moves slowly; small baits, long pauses.\\
40--45\,\unitof{^\circ F} & Late Mar--Apr & Ice-out. \textbf{Pike spawn} in flooded shallows --- the first fish to go. Walleye follow onto rock. Fish the north and west shorelines, which warm first.\\
45--55\,\unitof{^\circ F} & Apr--early May & \textbf{Walleye spawn} and then feed. Crappie push into bays. Slow presentations, live bait, dark bottoms that hold heat.\\
55--65\,\unitof{^\circ F} & May--early Jun & \textbf{Bass spawn}, largemouth first and smallmouth just after. Bass season opens the first Saturday in May. The single best window of the year for numbers of fish, shallow.\\
65--72\,\unitof{^\circ F} & Jun & \textbf{Panfish spawn} on colonial beds. Everything is shallow and visible. This is the month to take children.\\
72--80\,\unitof{^\circ F} & Jul--Aug & The lake stratifies. Fish split: some hold the deep weed edge, some go to the hard structure, and on Pine some suspend over the basin following cisco. Fish early, late, and deep.\\
72--60\,\unitof{^\circ F} & Sep & \textbf{Turnover.} The surface cools, the layers mix, and for one to two weeks the fishing is genuinely poor and the water may look cloudy. Wait it out.\\
60--45\,\unitof{^\circ F} & Oct--Nov & The best fishing of the year. Big fish feed heavily and move onto structure. Larger baits, and do not be afraid to fish the middle of the day.\\
45--33\,\unitof{^\circ F} & Nov--Dec & Deep and slow. Then first ice, which is often excellent --- and the most dangerous ice of the season.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\section{Times of day}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.16]
\telosfact{Dawn}{\telosbarrow{\telosdaybar{5,2,4,2}}\quad The most reliable hour on both lakes for almost everything. Non-negotiable in July and August.}
\telosfact{Midday}{Perch and rock bass feed; most predators do not. Bright calm midday is when finesse presentations earn their keep, \sheetref{wacky rig} and \sheetref{drop-shot}.}
\telosfact{Dusk}{The best hour for walleye and crappie. Be on the spot before the light goes rather than arriving with it.}
\telosfact{Night}{Walleye and big pike keep feeding; panfish shut down entirely.}
\telosfact{Weather}{A falling barometer ahead of a front turns fish on hard. The day after a cold front passes is the worst day of any week --- go deeper, slow down, and downsize.}
\telosfact{Wind}{Wind on a point or a bar concentrates plankton, then baitfish, then predators. Fish the windy shore, not the comfortable one.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telosframe}
\textbf{Season dates change every year.} On these waters in 2026--27, gamefish open 2 May and bass, walleye and pike close 7 March; musky is open water only through 31 December; panfish are open all year. Confirm with the Wisconsin DNR before every trip.
\end{telosframe}
