\telosmasthead[Learn these four and stop there]

Every knot weakens the line it is tied in; a good knot simply weakens it less.
Two habits matter more than which knot you choose: \textbf{wet it before you
pull it tight}, because friction burns and permanently weakens monofilament,
and \textbf{seat it slowly with steady pressure}, then look at it. A knot with
a crossed wrap is a knot that will fail on the best fish of the day.

\section{The four}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.19\linewidth}p{0.22\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Knot} & \textbf{Use it for} & \textbf{How to tie it}\\
\midrule
\textbf{Improved clinch} &
  Hook, swivel or lure to line. The everyday knot. &
  Pass the tag through the eye. Twist the tag around the standing line five to
  seven times. Pass the tag back through the small loop right at the eye, then
  \emph{back through the large loop} you just created --- that second pass is
  the ``improved'' part and it is what stops it slipping. Wet and pull the
  standing line slowly. Trim close.\\
\textbf{Palomar} &
  Braid to anything; the \sheetref{drop-shot}. Stronger than a clinch and harder
  to tie wrong. &
  Double about six inches of line and pass the doubled loop through the eye. Tie
  a loose overhand knot with the doubled line, leaving the hook hanging. Pass
  the loop completely \emph{over} the hook. Wet, then pull both the standing
  line and the tag to seat it. Trim.\\
\textbf{Uni (grinner)} &
  Leader to hook; and tied twice, back to back, it joins two lines of different
  diameter. &
  Pass the tag through the eye and lay it back alongside the standing line to
  form a loop. Wrap the tag through that loop and around both strands five or
  six times. Wet and pull the tag to close the coils, then slide the whole knot
  down to the eye.\\
\textbf{Double surgeon's} &
  Joining leader to main line quickly, including with cold hands. &
  Lay the two lines alongside each other, overlapping by six inches. Treating
  them as one, tie a simple overhand knot, then pass the same ends through a
  second time. Wet, and pull all four ends at once. Trim both tags.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\section{Getting them right}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Wet every knot}{Saliva or water, every time, before the final pull. A dry-cinched mono knot can lose a third of its strength to friction heat and you will never see the damage.}
\telosfact{Pull the right end}{On a clinch and a Palomar, seat the knot by pulling the \emph{standing line} steadily --- not by yanking the tag.}
\telosfact{Leave a short tag}{About 1/8 inch. Cutting flush lets the tag pull back through under load; leaving half an inch collects weed.}
\telosfact{Look at it}{Coils should lie in a neat barrel with no crossovers. If it looks wrong it is wrong. Cut it off and tie it again --- it takes twenty seconds.}
\telosfact{Retie often}{After every fish over a couple of pounds, after any snag, and after fishing rock. Run the last two feet of line between your fingers: if you feel roughness, cut it back and retie.}
\telosfact{Braid is different}{Braid is slippery. Use a Palomar, or add extra wraps to a uni. A standard clinch in braid will slip.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Two things that are not knots}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Snap swivel}{A fast way to change lures, and it lets a lure swing freely. But it is one more failure point and it is visible --- so on Pine Lake's clear water, tie direct.}
\telosfact{Wire leader}{Not optional for pike, \sheetref{Northern Pike}. Most are pre-made with a swivel and snap. If you tie your own, use a haywire twist rather than trying to knot single-strand wire.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{Teaching these}
Practise on rope or paracord first, at a size a child can see, before touching 6\,lb monofilament on a windy dock. Learn the improved clinch until it can be tied without looking, then the Palomar. Those two alone will cover everything on every rig sheet in this guide.
\end{telospanel}
