\telosmasthead[Plan for catching, not for fishing]

The failure mode of a first trip is always the same: an adult who wants to catch
a good fish takes a beginner to a place where good fish live, and the beginner
spends four hours not catching anything and concludes that fishing is boring.
They are right. Plan the opposite trip.

\section{The plan}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Target bluegill}{Not bass, not walleye, not "whatever bites". In June
  a bluegill bed will produce a fish every few minutes, and constant action is
  the entire point. \sheetref{Bluegill}}
\telosfact{Go in June}{Late May through June, when panfish are on the beds in
  1--4 ft of water and you can see them. Pine Lake spot \textbf{6}; North Lake
  spot \textbf{1}.}
\telosfact{Go in the evening}{Two hours before dark, not eight in the morning.
  Warmer, calmer, better bite, and a natural end to the trip.}
\telosfact{Keep it short}{Ninety minutes. Leave while it is still fun. The trip
  should end because you decided to end it, not because somebody has stopped
  enjoying it.}
\telosfact{One rod each}{A short spinning rod, 4\,lb line, a fixed bobber a
  couple of feet above a size 8 hook, half a worm. \sheetref{slip bobber} ---
  but use a fixed float, not a slip float, for the first trip.}
\telosfact{Pinch the barbs}{Flatten every barb with pliers. You will lose a few
  fish and you will save yourself the one memory that ends the hobby: a barbed
  hook in a finger, an hour from the car.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{What to bring}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=0.08em]
\item Rod, already rigged, at home
\item A spare rod, also already rigged
\item Worms, more than you think
\item Long-nose pliers
\item Nail clippers for line
\item Landing net
\item Wet towel
\item Life jackets that fit \emph{them}
\item Sunscreen and a hat
\item Bug spray
\item Snacks and water --- more than the fishing gear, honestly
\item Something to keep fish in, if keeping
\item A first-aid kit with tweezers
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}

\section{The first hour, in order}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Rig both rods at home.
\item Pinch each barb flat with pliers.
\item Pull each knot hard.
\item Cover each hook for transport.
\item Put the fitted life jacket on the beginner.
\item Put your own life jacket on.
\item Clip a hookless casting plug to one rod.
\item Stand on grass with everyone behind the caster.
\item Demonstrate one short underhand flip.
\item Let the beginner make five flips.
\item Stop practice when the plug lands forward without crossing anyone.
\item Replace the plug with the covered fishing rig.
\item Uncover the hook only at the fishing position.
\item Set the float so the bait rides just above the visible bed.
\item Let the beginner bait, cast, watch, and reel.
\item Hand over the spare rod immediately when the first rod tangles.
\item Net the first fish at water level.
\item Wet both sets of hands.
\item Support the fish horizontally.
\item Identify one visible feature.
\item Release the fish before preparing the next cast.
\item Leave at 90 minutes or while the beginner still wants one more cast,
whichever comes first.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{telospanel}{Checks and stop branches}
Practice passes only when every cast travels forward and the hookless plug
stays clear of people. Fishing passes only when the life jacket remains fitted,
the hook is controlled, and the adult can reach the beginner. If thunder,
strong wind, unsafe footing, cold stress, panic, or repeated hook-control loss
appears, stop and leave. If no fish contacts the bait in 15 minutes, shorten
the depth once; after another 15 minutes, move once or switch to looking for
aquatic life rather than extending the trip.
\end{telospanel}

\section{Things that go wrong, and the fix}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.24\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Problem} & \textbf{Fix}\\
\midrule
Tangles & Inevitable. Carry the pre-rigged spare rod, hand it over, and untangle
  the first one later. Do not make a child stand and watch you pick at a bird's
  nest for ten minutes.\\
Nothing biting & You are in the wrong spot or too deep. Move shallower, go
  smaller, use less bait. Half a worm out-fishes a whole one for panfish every
  time.\\
Bored & The bite has slowed. Change activity rather than persisting: look at
  what is in the water, catch a crayfish under a rock, throw the cast net, go
  home. Fishing is not the only good thing about being at a lake.\\
Hook in a finger & This is why the barbs are pinched. Back it out the way it
  went in, clean it, plaster on it, carry on. With a barb still on, cut the line
  and see a doctor.\\
Frightened of the fish & Normal. Hold it yourself first, let them touch it while
  you hold it, and do not push. They will pick one up within a couple of trips.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{telospanel}{The measure of success}
Not the size of the fish, and not how many. The measure is whether they ask to
go again. Everything on this sheet is arranged to make that answer yes.
\end{telospanel}
