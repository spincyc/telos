\begin{eatingfacts}
\telosfact{Verdict}{Not a fish to eat, and this is the one sheet in the guide that says so. \telosrating{1}}
\telosfact{Mercury}{Muskellunge sit at the top of the food chain and live for fifteen to twenty years, so they accumulate more mercury than anything else in the lake. Wisconsin's statewide guidance is \textbf{do not eat} for women under 50 and children under 15, and one serving per \emph{month} for everybody else. No other species in this guide carries a do-not-eat line.}
\telosfact{Scarcity}{Musky are not among the species the DNR lists as common in either lake. A musky here is a rare, old, slow-growing fish.}
\telosfact{Regulation}{40\,in minimum, one per day, open water only. A legal musky is a very large animal.}
\telosfact{If you must}{The flesh is white and firm, comparable to pike and with the same Y-bone structure. \sheetref{Cooking Northern Pike}}
\telosfact{Better outcome}{Photograph it in the net, measure it, and let it go.}
\end{eatingfacts}

\section{If you catch one}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Confirm it is a musky and not a pike} before you do anything else. Dark marks on a light body, the lower half of both cheek and gill cover scaleless, six to nine pores per side of the lower jaw. \sheetref{Muskellunge}
\item \textbf{Measure it in the net, in the water.} Under 40\,in it goes back regardless.
\item \textbf{Keep it horizontal and wet.} A large musky is injured by its own weight in air. Support the belly.
\item \textbf{Unhook it in the water} with long pliers and jaw spreaders. Cut the hook if it is awkward.
\item \textbf{Revive it properly} --- upright, moving forward, until it swims off under its own power. A big musky may need several minutes.
\item \textbf{Photograph it low over the water}, so that if it kicks free it lands in the lake and not on the deck.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bones and what to watch for}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Same as pike}{Y-bones above the lateral line in the front of each fillet, floating and un-pullable.}
\telosfact{But}{The bone structure is not the reason to leave it. The mercury load and the rarity of the fish are.}
\telosfact{Handling}{Teeth as bad as a pike's on a much larger animal. Jaw spreaders and long pliers, always.}
\telosfact{Gear}{A landing net big enough for the fish, ready before you hook one.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{Why this sheet exists}
It would be easy to leave musky out of a cooking guide entirely. But somebody will eventually catch a legal one and want to know, and the honest answer is a number: Wisconsin's health guidance puts musky in a category by itself, alongside no other inland species. That is worth knowing before the fish is in the cooler rather than after.
\end{telospanel}
