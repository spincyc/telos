\begin{telosfacts}[0.26]
\telosfact{What it does}{The float slides freely on the line until it hits the stop knot. You can fish 20 ft down and still reel the float to the rod tip to cast.}
\telosfact{Why it matters}{A fixed bobber can only fish as deep as your rod is long. A slip float has no such limit, which is the whole reason it exists.}
\telosfact{Components}{Bobber stop (thread or rubber), small bead, slip float, one or two split shot, hook.}
\telosfact{Hook}{Size 8--10 for panfish, 4--6 for crappie on a minnow, 1/0 for bass or walleye on a leech.}
\telosfact{Line}{4\,lb for panfish; 6--8\,lb for bass and walleye.}
\telosfact{Catches}{Bluegill, pumpkinseed, crappie, perch, largemouth, walleye, pike.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Build it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Thread the \textbf{bobber stop} onto the main line and slide it up. Tighten it enough to hold but loose enough to slide when you pull --- this is what you will adjust all day.
\item Thread the \textbf{bead}. Its only job is to stop the small stop knot from pulling through the float's hole.
\item Thread the \textbf{float}, top end first.
\item Pinch on \textbf{split shot} 8--12 in above where the hook will be. Enough weight that the float sits upright with only its tip showing --- an over-floated bobber cannot signal a bite.
\item Tie on the \textbf{hook} with an improved clinch knot, \sheetref{knots}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Fish it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Set the depth}{Bait should ride just above whatever the fish are holding on --- the weed tops, the bottom, the school. Fish look up far more readily than down. Start a foot above and work down.}
\telosfact{Read it}{A bluegill takes the float sideways and down in small taps. A crappie lifts it and lays it flat --- that is a fish swimming up with the bait, and it is a bite. Walleye pull it under slowly and steadily.}
\telosfact{Set the hook}{Reel the slack, then sweep. Do not snap a rod at a crappie, \sheetref{Black Crappie}.}
\telosfact{Adjust}{A float that lies flat and stays flat is too deep --- the bait is on the bottom. Slide the stop down.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{When to use it}
Any time you know the depth and want the bait to stay there: over a weed edge, over a crappie school suspended at 14 ft, over a bluegill bed, or with a big sucker over a weed flat for pike. It is the most controllable live-bait presentation there is, and it is the easiest rig in this guide to hand to a beginner.
\end{telospanel}
