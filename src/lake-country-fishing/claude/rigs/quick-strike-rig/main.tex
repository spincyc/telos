\begin{telosfacts}[0.26]
\telosfact{What it does}{Two small treble hooks set into the flank of a large dead bait, on a wire trace. Because the hooks are on the \emph{outside} of the bait, you strike immediately instead of waiting for the pike to swallow.}
\telosfact{Why it matters}{The old method --- a single hook inside a bait, and a long wait --- gut-hooks pike and kills fish you meant to release. A quick-strike rig hooks them in the jaw. This is the humane way to do it, and it is also more effective.}
\telosfact{Components}{Wire trace with two size 6--8 trebles, barrel swivel, slip float and stop, large dead bait.}
\telosfact{Bait}{A dead sucker, smelt or large shiner, 6--10\,in. Dead bait works: pike scavenge readily, especially in cold water.}
\telosfact{Wire}{Not optional. Pike teeth cut monofilament instantly.}
\telosfact{Line}{20\,lb braid or 15\,lb mono; a rod with backbone.}
\end{telosfacts}
\end{minipage}

\section{Build and bait it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Rig the \textbf{slip float} on the main line --- stop knot, bead, float --- exactly as on the \sheetref{slip bobber} sheet. Set the depth so the bait hangs just above the weed tops.
\item Tie the main line to a \textbf{barrel swivel}, and the swivel to the \textbf{wire trace}.
\item Set the \textbf{rear treble} into the bait's flank near the tail, one point only, barely under the skin.
\item Set the \textbf{front treble} near the head, again one point only. The bait should hang naturally, not bent.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Fish it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Strike early}{When the float goes down, count to \textbf{five}, reel until tight, and sweep hard. Do not give it a minute. Do not give it thirty seconds. The whole design assumes an early strike.}
\telosfact{Tackle at hand}{Long-nose pliers, side cutters and jaw spreaders in the boat before you cast, not after you hook one.}
\telosfact{Cut, do not dig}{If a hook is awkward, cut the treble with side cutters and back the pieces out. A cheap hook is worth less than the fish.}
\telosfact{Where}{Pine spots \textbf{1} and \textbf{9} in April and again in October; the deep weed edge at \textbf{5} in summer. North spots \textbf{2}, \textbf{5} and \textbf{6}.}
\telosfact{Ice}{The same rig on a tip-up is the standard big-pike presentation, \sheetref{tip-up}.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{teloshazard}{Handle pike properly}
Backward-facing teeth, and a fish strong enough to thrash while you work. Jaw spreaders and long pliers every time; fingers never inside the mouth or under the gill plate. Pike over \qty{70}{^\circ F} water are fragile --- unhook in the net, in the water, and release quickly. See the \textbf{Northern Pike} sheet.
\end{teloshazard}
