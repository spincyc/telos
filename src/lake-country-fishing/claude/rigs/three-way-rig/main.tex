\begin{telosfacts}[0.26]
\telosfact{What it does}{Three lines meet at one swivel: the main line up to the rod, a short dropper down to the weight, and a leader out to the bait. The weight tracks the bottom while the bait rides at a fixed height above it.}
\telosfact{Versus lindy}{A lindy rig lets a fish take line freely. A three-way keeps a rigid, known geometry on a steep slope where a lindy rig would tumble down the face.}
\telosfact{Components}{Three-way swivel, 8--18\,in dropper, 1/2--1\,oz bell sinker, 2--4\,ft leader, single hook or small crankbait.}
\telosfact{Dropper line}{Deliberately \emph{lighter} than the main line. When the sinker wedges in rock, you break off a sinker and keep the fish, the leader and the bait.}
\telosfact{Bait}{Big minnow, a crawler, or a small floating crankbait that rides up off the bottom.}
\telosfact{Catches}{Walleye, pike, big smallmouth.}
\end{telosfacts}
\end{minipage}

\section{Build it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Tie the \textbf{main line} to the top eye of the three-way swivel.
\item Tie a short \textbf{dropper} of lighter line to the bottom eye, and the \textbf{sinker} to its end. Make the dropper as long as you want the bait to ride above the bottom.
\item Tie the \textbf{leader} to the third eye, then the hook or lure to the leader.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Fish it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Troll or drift slowly}{Move at a walking pace with the sinker just ticking bottom. The rig is designed to be moving.}
\telosfact{Vertical line}{Keep the line close to straight down. Let line out until you touch bottom, then take up a few inches. Re-check constantly on a slope --- that is the whole point of the rig.}
\telosfact{Feel for the change}{You are following a contour. When the sinker stops touching, you have moved deep; when it drags hard, you have moved shallow. Steer to keep contact.}
\telosfact{Set}{A steady sweep. The fish usually hooks itself against the moving boat.}
\telosfact{Where}{Pine spot \textbf{3} --- the east wall is the exact situation this rig was invented for --- and the steep side of the bar at \textbf{4}. North spot \textbf{3}.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{When to use it}
On a drop too steep to fish any other way. If you keep losing bottom contact and then plowing, alternately, on a sharp break, this is the rig. It also lets you fish a floating crankbait a fixed 18 inches off the rocks, which is deadly on fall walleye.
\end{telospanel}
\rigfieldproof{Pull all three knots separately; measure the 8--18\,in dropper and 2--4\,ft leader, with the weakest line on the sinker branch.}{Tow beside the boat: sinker branch hangs below while the bait leader streams clear without wrapping.}{The sinker taps continuously enough to map the face while the bait remains above it.}{Branches twist: shorten the bait leader or lower more slowly. No taps: add weight. Repeated sinker loss is acceptable only if the weak dropper breaks cleanly.}
