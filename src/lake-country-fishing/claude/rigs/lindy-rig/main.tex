\begin{telosfacts}[0.26]
\telosfact{What it does}{The sinker drags the bottom; the line slides \emph{through} it. When a fish picks up the bait it feels no weight at all, so it keeps moving instead of dropping the bait.}
\telosfact{Why it matters}{A cold-water walleye mouths a bait and spits anything that resists. This rig removes the resistance.}
\telosfact{Components}{Walking or egg sinker, bead, barrel swivel, 3--6 ft leader, single hook.}
\telosfact{Weight}{1/4\,oz to 20 ft, 3/8\,oz to 30 ft, 1/2\,oz deeper or in wind. Use the lightest that keeps bottom contact.}
\telosfact{Leader}{Long and light in Pine Lake's clear water --- 6 ft of 6\,lb fluorocarbon. Shorter and heavier on North Lake.}
\telosfact{Bait}{Leech, half a nightcrawler, or a lively minnow hooked through the lips.}
\end{telosfacts}
\end{minipage}

\section{Build it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Thread the \textbf{sinker} onto the main line, narrow end first, so the line runs freely through it.
\item Add a \textbf{bead} to protect the knot from the sinker.
\item Tie the main line to one eye of a \textbf{barrel swivel}. The swivel is the stop: the sinker rides above it and cannot reach the hook.
\item Tie the \textbf{leader} to the other eye, then the \textbf{hook} to the leader.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Fish it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Speed}{Slower than feels right. Drift with the wind or use the electric motor at a crawl --- roughly the speed you would walk a dog that keeps stopping.}
\telosfact{Contact}{You must feel the sinker ticking bottom. If you cannot, add weight; if it is plowing, take weight off.}
\telosfact{The take}{You will feel weight, or a tap, or simply nothing where there was bottom a moment ago. \textbf{Do not set the hook.}}
\telosfact{Feed it}{Open the bail or drop the rod tip and give the fish line for a slow count of five to ten. The fish is turning the bait to swallow it head first.}
\telosfact{Then set}{Close the bail, reel until the line comes tight, and sweep the rod. No snap.}
\telosfact{Follow the contour}{Keep the boat on one depth line. A walleye break is a line, not a spot.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{When to use it}
Pine Lake spots \textbf{3} and \textbf{8}, North Lake spot \textbf{3}, in 18--35 ft. It is the highest-percentage way to catch a walleye on either lake, and it doubles as a smallmouth and perch rig. It is also the rig that most rewards patience: the whole thing depends on giving line to a fish that has not yet decided to keep your bait.
\end{telospanel}
\rigfieldproof{Pull both swivel knots, slide the sinker freely, and measure the leader at 3--6 ft rather than by arm span.}{Tow beside the boat: sinker walks bottom while bait follows upright and does not spin.}{With the rod low, feel intermittent ticks and confirm the sinker still slides when line is fed.}{No ticks: add the next weight size. Bait spins: rehook it straight or slow down. Frequent weed: shorten the leader.}
