\begin{telospanel}{Choose from conditions, not reputation}
Pine and North are not interchangeable. Pick the water whose structure matches
the day, then use the first hour to test a simple prediction. This is a search
plan, not a promise of fish.
\end{telospanel}

\section{Pick in sixty seconds}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{teloscard}{Choose Pine when}
\begin{itemize}
\item you can search depth efficiently;
\item low light or broken surface reduces visibility;
\item you want to test steep breaks, hard-bottom edges, or suspended marks;
\item your boat and crew suit a larger open-water search.
\end{itemize}
\end{teloscard}
\columnbreak
\begin{teloscard}{Choose North when}
\begin{itemize}
\item wind makes a smaller search footprint attractive;
\item you want to test mixed-bottom transitions, weeds, necks, or inflow;
\item you can move quietly through confined water;
\item traffic and posted local rules allow the plan.
\end{itemize}
\end{teloscard}
\end{multicols}

\begin{teloshazard}{Conditions over card}
Check the live forecast, launch status, posted ordinances, and current

\end{teloshazard}

\section{Make one prediction}
Write one sentence before launching:
\begin{telosframe}
\textbf{Because} \rule{0.28\linewidth}{0.4pt}, \textbf{fish should be}
\rule{0.24\linewidth}{0.4pt}, \textbf{so I will test}
\rule{0.24\linewidth}{0.4pt} \textbf{until} \rule{0.15\linewidth}{0.4pt}.
\end{telosframe}

Example: ``Wind has pushed food onto a gravel edge, so I will make three
controlled passes.'' A prediction makes moving useful; random movement only
changes scenery.

\section{The first-hour search}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Spend 10 minutes observing: temperature, wind lane, clarity, weeds,
surface activity, and marks.
\item Fish one structural idea at two depths or speeds for 20 minutes.
\item Change \emph{one} variable and repeat for 20 minutes.
\item At 50 minutes, choose: refine, change pattern, or leave.
\end{enumerate}
