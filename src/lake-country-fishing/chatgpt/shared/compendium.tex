\section*{Field-learning loop}
This binder is a set of claims to test, not a promise that every candidate
species or tactic will occur. Move through the evidence in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Lake sheet:} locate access, contours, evidence limits, and the
dated rule summary.
\item \textbf{Fish sheet:} identify the catch from diagnostic structure before
assigning a species name.
\item \textbf{Rig and reference sheets:} choose a presentation and record the
variables that can change.
\item \textbf{Kitchen sheet:} use only after lawful retention; identification
and current consumption advice come first.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Trip checkpoints}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{.18\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
When & Verify, then decide\\
\midrule
At launch & Access is lawful; wind, weather, equipment, and return plan remain
safe.\\
After 30 minutes & Record depth and presentation. Change one variable, or move
to the next mapped structure; do not rewrite the species evidence.\\
At first contact & Photograph diagnostic features when safe, measure length,
and separate observation from identification.\\
Before retention & Confirm species, live rule, size, bag, meal advice, cold
storage, and intended use.\\
Before return & Recheck weather, battery or fuel, daylight, and the safest
route to access.\\

\begin{teloshazard}{Do not promote a field note into a lake record}
A photograph or catch can support identification of that individual. It does
not by itself establish abundance, persistence, a fishable population, or the
status of a different species. Keep candidate and historical modules in their
printed evidence class until a current authoritative record changes it.
\end{teloshazard}
