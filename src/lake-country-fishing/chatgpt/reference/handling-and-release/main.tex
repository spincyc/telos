\section{Build the release station}
Match tackle to the largest plausible fish. Open pliers and cutters; wet and
stage a knotless rubber net; set the camera before casting. Decide whether the
fish can remain beside the boat.

\begin{telospanel}{Before-cast check}
Pliers open one-handed; cutters sever the actual leader; net is untangled and
wet; measuring board is ready; camera is awake. If release is likely, favor
tackle that limits deep hooking.
\end{telospanel}

\section{At the fish: decide at each gate}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Control.} Use steady pressure and adequate tackle. If the fish
still surges, keep it in water; do not drag it onto a dry floor.
\item \textbf{Net or boatside?} Net only when it reduces handling. Guide the
fish headfirst; never chase its tail with the hoop.
\item \textbf{Hook access?} Back out an exposed hook with the fish supported.
If deep extraction is slow or destructive, cut the line promptly.
\item \textbf{Photo?} Only if camera and hands are ready. Lift horizontally,
close to the water, then return it without delay.
\item \textbf{Release.} Support upright in water. Let go when it holds balance
and swims under its own power; face gentle current only when one exists.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Ask mid-process:} Is the next action for the fish, or for the picture?
Has air exposure stopped being brief? Would cutting now cause less injury?

\section{Troubleshooting the release}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}p{.25\linewidth}X@{}}
\toprule Observation&Response\\\midrule
Fish rolls on its side&Keep supported upright in water; reduce handling. If
legal harvest is not an option, give it quiet time rather than pumping.\\
Hook will not reverse&Expose only what is necessary; cut hook or line with
staged cutters instead of tearing tissue.\\
Fish repeatedly caught deep&Change target depth, location, tackle, or species;
do not keep proving the same harm.\\
Warm fish fights poorly&Shorten the fight and air time; consider ending or
changing the trip before another capture.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\begin{teloshazard}{Depth and temperature}
Deep capture and warm water increase risk. Wisconsin DNR does not recommend
artificial swim-bladder deflation. Change location, depth, tackle, or target
rather than repeatedly producing fish unlikely to survive.
\end{teloshazard}
\textbf{Release verified:} the fish remains upright without hand pressure,
shows coordinated fin movement, and leaves under its own power.
