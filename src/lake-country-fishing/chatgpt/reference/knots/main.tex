\begin{teloshazard}{Control hooks and stored energy}
Point hooks away from faces and hands. Wear eye protection when testing or
pulling free. Cut snagged line under control; never snap it toward the boat.
\end{teloshazard}

\section{Choose the knot before tying}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}p{.18\linewidth}p{.29\linewidth}X@{}}
\toprule Knot&Use when&Do not choose when\\\midrule
Improved clinch&Small hook or lure eye; mono/fluoro; familiar general knot&Very
heavy or exceptionally slippery line without testing.\\
Palomar&Doubled line fits through eye; high strength with few wraps&Large lure
cannot pass through the loop cleanly.\\
Uni&Versatile terminal connection; easy to inspect and slide&Wraps cannot be
kept parallel around very stiff material.\\
Surgeon's loop&Fast fixed end loop for loop-to-loop leaders&A compact terminal
knot directly against a hook eye is required.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\section{Improved clinch}
\knotsteps{Pass tag through eye; leave 6--8 in}{Make 5--7 neat turns around
standing line}{Return through eye-side loop, then new large loop}{Wet; pull tag
to close, standing line to seat}
\textbf{Look for:} a compact barrel of parallel coils ending against the eye.
\textbf{Reject if:} one wrap crosses the others, coils show white flattening,
or the tag retreats during the pull test.

\section{Palomar}
\knotsteps{Double 6--8 in; pass loop through eye}{Tie a loose overhand with the
doubled line}{Pass the entire hook or lure through loop}{Wet; draw all four
strands evenly, then seat}
\textbf{Intermediate question:} Did the loop pass over the tackle without
twisting around a hook point? Before seating, both strands through the eye must
lie side by side, not cross.

\newpage
\section{Uni knot}
\knotsteps{Pass through eye; lay tag beside standing line}{Turn tag back to form
an open loop}{Wrap tag through loop 5--7 times}{Wet; close coils, then slide
the knot to eye}
\textbf{Measurement:} begin with 8--10 in of tag while learning. The finished
coil should be roughly one line-diameter stack per wrap, not a loose spiral.

\section{Surgeon's loop}
\knotsteps{Double 8 in of line}{Form a loose overhand loop}{Pass doubled end
through the opening twice}{Wet; pull both doubled legs evenly}
\textbf{Look for:} two turns seated together and a loop that remains open
without one leg slipping. Trim only after loading both legs.

\section{The repeatable finish}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Wet mono/fluoro; keep braid wraps controlled.
\item Close the knot slowly while guiding coils with fingers.
\item Pull standing line and tag firmly; then load the tackle connection.
\item Inspect under light from both sides. Trim safely, leaving a short tag.
\item Test harder than the presentation load, away from face and hook path.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}p{.25\linewidth}X@{}}
\toprule Failure&Correction\\\midrule
Knot slips&Retie with adequate tag and correct wrap count; do not “fix” by
melting a tag end.\\
Line curls or whitens&Friction damaged it. Cut above the damaged section, wet,
and seat more slowly.\\
Break occurs above knot&Inspect guides and line for abrasion; remove enough
line to reach undamaged material.\\
Knot looks different each time&Use the same tag length and count wraps aloud
until the sequence is automatic.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\begin{telospanel}{Retie now}
Retie after abrasion, snags, tooth contact, a day of repeated casting, or any
unexplained failure. A passing knot is compact, undamaged, motionless under a
firm pull, and reproducible twice in succession.
\end{telospanel}
