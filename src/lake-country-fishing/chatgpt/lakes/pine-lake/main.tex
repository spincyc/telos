\telosmasthead[Waukesha County, Wisconsin]

Pine is a large, deep spring lake with an overwhelmingly gravel bottom. Its
1955 survey shows sharp basin edges, a mid-lake island and long pieces of hard
structure. Use this sheet to form a search plan, then let lawful electronics,
visible vegetation, weather and bites revise it.

\section{A disciplined Pine sequence}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Before launch, read the posted ordinance and access signs and check the
exact-water DNR regulation lookup.
\item Measure clarity and surface temperature. On bright, calm days begin
farther from the edge and make longer casts.
\item Search one shallow area, one break and one basin-adjacent feature. Record
depth, bottom, vegetation and bait before changing lure.
\item Treat historical cisco evidence as a reason to scan open water, not as
proof that a current cisco population or suspended predator bite exists.
\item Clean, drain and remove plants and animals before leaving; Pine has
multiple recorded aquatic invasive species.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teloshazard}{Historical map, current water}
The source contours are more than seventy years old and generalized here.
Water level, vegetation, hazards, docks and access can change. Navigate with
current lawful aids and direct observation, never this sheet.
\end{teloshazard}

\section{Measure the water consistently}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Choose stations.} Use the same three Pine stations each trip:
one protected shelf, one wind-exposed break and one basin-adjacent point.
Write the map number or a lawful GPS waypoint before measuring.
\item \textbf{Depth.} Stop safely outside traffic. Record sonar depth only
after the reading stabilizes; then repeat from the opposite heading. If using
a marked sounding line, stop propulsion, wear a PFD and keep the line clear of
hands, feet and propeller.
\item \textbf{Temperature.} Shade the thermometer. Record surface temperature
after it stabilizes. Lower it to 5, 10 and 20 ft where the station is deep
enough, pausing at each mark. Write ``not measured'' rather than estimating.
\item \textbf{Clarity.} On the shaded side, lower a standard black-and-white
Secchi disk until it disappears; mark depth. Raise it until it reappears; mark
again. Record the average and repeat once. Do not lean overboard in waves.
\item \textbf{Context.} Record wind direction/strength, sky, recent rain,
surface debris, vegetation height and whether boat traffic affected the test.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{telospanel}{Comparable beats precise-looking}
Use the same tool, side of the boat, units and station each trip. A repeatable
rough measurement is more useful than a decimal copied from unstable gear.
Never infer oxygen from temperature alone.
\end{telospanel}

\subsection{Verification questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Did two depth passes agree within \rule{.45in}{.4pt} ft? If not, what
changed: heading, waves, weeds, bottom slope or instrument setting?
\item Was the Secchi average deeper or shallower than last trip by
\rule{.45in}{.4pt} ft? Name one observation that could explain the change.
\item At what depth did temperature change fastest? \rule{.7in}{.4pt}.
Did bait marks occur above, within or below that change? \rule{1in}{.4pt}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teloshazard}{A forecast is not clearance}
Check before departure and again at the water. If observed wind, lightning,
visibility, waves, access or posted restrictions exceed the people or craft,
do not launch or return immediately. A planned fishing test never outranks a
safe return.
\end{teloshazard}

\section{Evidence boundary}

The current DNR lake record supports panfish, largemouth bass, smallmouth bass,
northern pike and walleye. Older agency-derived reports also record cisco,
common carp and two shiners, but historical presence is not current abundance.
The provider-neutral evidence record is
\texttt{research/lake-country-fishing/lakes-pine.md}.
