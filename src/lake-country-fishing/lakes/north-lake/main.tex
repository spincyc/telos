\telosmasthead[Town of Merton]

North Lake sits two miles north of Pine Lake and fishes almost nothing like it.
It is a river lake: the Oconomowoc River enters the south-west lobe and leaves
at the north-east corner, and that flow keeps the water stained where Pine Lake
is glass. Lower clarity is an advantage. Fish here hold shallower, feed later
into the morning, and are far less leader-shy. The lake is also two lakes ---
a long main basin and a round south-west lobe, each with its own deep hole near
78 ft, joined by one narrow neck that every fish in the system has to swim
through.

\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Surface}{440 acres (449 acres on the 1898 survey); 3.44 miles of shoreline}
\telosfact{Depth}{78.4 ft maximum. Two separate holes: one in each basin}
\telosfact{Clarity}{Low. Stained, river-fed water --- shorter weed line, shallower fish,
  more forgiving of heavier line}
\telosfact{Hydrology}{Oconomowoc River in at the west end of the lobe, out at the
  north-east corner. Mud Lake lies immediately south}
\telosfact{Fish}{Panfish, largemouth bass, smallmouth bass and northern pike (all
  common), walleye (present)}
\telosfact{Access}{No municipal ramp. Carry-in public access in the Town of Merton;
  a DNR fishing pier and small-craft launch have been planned. \textbf{Verify
  what exists and what it costs before you tow anything.} Practically, this is
  a canoe, kayak or car-top lake}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{The seven spots, by season}

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{0.045\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.19\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.40\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{Spot} & \textbf{When \& what} & \textbf{How}\\
\midrule
\endhead
1 & North-end flat &
  \textbf{Apr--Jun} panfish, largemouth, pike; \textbf{Dec--Feb} panfish under ice. &
  The broad shallow end of the main basin, under 30 ft nearly all the way across.
  Weed flats that warm first. Slip bobber at 4--10 ft; spinnerbait over the tops
  for pike.\\
2 & North-east outlet corner &
  \textbf{Apr--May} and \textbf{Sep--Nov} pike and panfish. &
  Where the lake drains to the river. Moving water concentrates baitfish and the
  things that eat them. Fish the current seam. Quick-strike rig with a big
  sucker; jig-and-minnow on the edge.\\
3 & East shoreline drop &
  \textbf{Jun--Sep} smallmouth and walleye; \textbf{Oct} pike. &
  The main basin's east side falls off steadily to the 78 ft hole. Work the
  15--30 ft band. Drop-shot, or a lindy rig dragged along the contour.\\
4 & \textbf{South-end shoal complex} &
  \textbf{May--Oct}, everything. &
  Hard humps topping out near 4 ft with deep water all around --- the best
  structure in the lake. Smallmouth and walleye stack on the edges; pike patrol
  the tops. Start shallow at dawn, work down to 20--25 ft by mid-morning.\\
5 & \textbf{The neck} &
  \textbf{All open water.} Pike, bass, walleye, panfish. &
  The only connection between the two basins: a shallow, current-swept
  bottleneck. Everything that moves between basins passes through. Anchor off
  the edge and let bait sit; or cast a jig across and swing it with the current.\\
6 & River inlet, west end of the lobe &
  \textbf{Mar--May} and \textbf{Oct--Nov} pike; panfish and bass in summer. &
  Incoming river water, warmest in spring and coldest in fall. Stained and
  weedy. Pike stage here before and after the spawn. Big bait, slow.\\
7 & South shore of the lobe &
  \textbf{Jun--Sep} bass and panfish deep; \textbf{Jul--Aug} suspended fish. &
  A short, steep drop into the lobe's own 78 ft hole. Fish the 12--25 ft break;
  in high summer look for fish suspended off the edge rather than on it.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\section{How North Lake differs from Pine Lake}

If you fish both, do not carry one lake's habits to the other.

\begin{telosfacts}[0.24]
\telosfact{Fish shallower}{Stained water means the weed line ends around
  8--12 ft instead of 15--20 ft. The whole food chain moves up with it.}
\telosfact{Fish later}{Low-light advantage is smaller here. A cloudy mid-morning
  on North Lake can beat dawn.}
\telosfact{Fish heavier}{You can get away with heavier line, bigger baits and
  visible hardware. Save the fluorocarbon and the finesse for Pine.}
\telosfact{Two lakes}{Each basin has its own population behaviour. If the main
  basin is dead, cross the neck and fish the lobe --- it is a different water.}
\telosfact{The river matters}{Spring warm-up, fall cool-down and after heavy
  rain, the inflow and outflow are the first places to look.}
\telosfact{Getting there}{No trailer ramp. That is why it stays quiet, and it is
  the best reason to fish it.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{teloshazard}{Check the regulations before every trip}
The 2026--27 rules for this water are the same as Pine Lake's: largemouth and
smallmouth bass 14\,in minimum, 5 per day, 2 May--7 March, catch-and-release the
rest of the year; walleye 18\,in minimum, 3 per day; northern pike 26\,in
minimum, 2 per day; muskellunge 40\,in minimum, 1 per day, open water only
through 31 December; panfish 25 per day; cisco and whitefish 10 per day.
\textbf{These change.} The Wisconsin DNR publishes the current rules for this
exact water body, and only that publication is authoritative.
\end{teloshazard}
