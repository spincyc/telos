Every numbered spot in this guide was chosen by the same reasoning, and once you
can do that reasoning yourself you no longer need the numbers. Fish are not
distributed evenly. They stack up wherever two things meet.

\section{The one idea: fish live on edges}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{What an edge is}{Any place where the water changes --- shallow to
  deep, weed to open, hard bottom to soft, warm to cold, bright to shaded,
  current to still. An edge gives a predator cover on one side and hunting
  ground on the other, and it concentrates the food chain from the bottom up.}
\telosfact{The best spots have two}{A weed edge is good. A weed edge on a point
  is better. A weed edge on a point next to a fast drop into deep water is where
  everything in the lake eventually shows up. Pine Lake's mid-lake bar is four
  edges stacked in one place, which is why it is spot \textbf{4}.}
\telosfact{The featureless middle}{A flat, uniform bottom in open water holds
  almost nothing. If you cannot name the edge you are fishing, you are probably
  not fishing anything.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{The invisible edge: the thermocline}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{What happens}{By early summer a deep lake settles into three layers:
  a warm mixed layer on top, a thin band where temperature falls sharply --- the
  thermocline --- and cold, dense water below.}
\telosfact{Why it matters}{The layers stop mixing. Below the thermocline, oxygen
  is no longer being replenished. In most lakes that makes the deep water
  uninhabitable by midsummer, so \emph{every fish in the lake} is squeezed into
  the water above it.}
\telosfact{Pine Lake is the exception}{It is deep and clean enough to keep
  oxygen in the cold layer, which is why it holds cisco. That means predators
  here have somewhere to go that most lakes do not --- and in July and August
  they go there. \sheetref{Cisco}}
\telosfact{How to find it}{On sonar it is often a faint horizontal band of
  clutter. Or lower a thermometer: where the reading drops several degrees in a
  couple of feet, that is it. Typically 15--25 ft in this part of Wisconsin.}
\telosfact{Turnover}{In autumn the surface cools until the layers have the same
  density and the whole lake mixes. For a week or two the water looks cloudy and
  the fishing is genuinely poor. Then it is the best month of the year.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{A method for a lake you have never seen}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Look at a contour map \emph{before} you launch and mark every point, bar,
  inside turn and narrows. That is your list of candidate spots and it costs
  nothing.
\item On the water, find the weed line first. It tells you how far light
  penetrates, which tells you how deep everything else will be.
\item Start shallow and early. Work out and down as the sun climbs.
\item Fish an edge, not an area. Keep the boat on one depth and follow it.
\item When you catch one, stop and note the depth, the bottom, and the edge.
  Then find the same combination somewhere else on the lake --- fish are
  creatures of pattern, and the pattern repeats.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{telospanel}{The point of the numbered spots}
The nine spots on Pine Lake and the seven on North Lake are not secrets; they
are worked examples. Each one is a named edge, and the lake sheets say which
edge and why. Learn the reasoning and you can walk up to any lake in the county
and produce your own list before you get the boat wet.
\end{telospanel}
