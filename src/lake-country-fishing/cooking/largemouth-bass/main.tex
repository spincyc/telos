\begin{eatingfacts}
\telosfact{Verdict}{Much better than anglers say, provided the water was cold. A bass from 50\,\unitof{^\circ F} water in April is clean and sweet; the same fish in August can taste of weed and mud. \telosrating{3}}
\telosfact{Texture}{Firm and meaty, larger flake than walleye, holds up to grilling and to a heavier sauce.}
\telosfact{The mud line}{Flavour lives in the dark red lateral strip of muscle just under the skin. Trim it off and most of the complaint goes with it.}
\telosfact{Best size}{14--17\,in. A big old bass is a breeder and coarse eating --- put it back.}
\telosfact{Yield}{About 30\% as skinless fillet. A 15\,in bass gives two fillets of roughly 4\,oz.}
\telosfact{Season}{Keep bass in spring and autumn; release them in high summer. This is a flavour judgement, not just a conservation one.}
\end{eatingfacts}

\section{Filleting}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cut down behind the gill plate} and the pectoral fin, angling slightly toward the head, until the blade meets the backbone. Do not cut through it.
\item \textbf{Turn the blade flat} and run it along the top of the backbone to the tail in one long stroke, letting the spine guide the knife. Leave the fillet attached at the tail so it acts as a handle.
\item \textbf{Slice the rib cage out} in a shallow arc, taking as little flesh with it as you can.
\item \textbf{Skin it:} fillet skin-side down, grip the tail, run the knife between skin and flesh at a slight downward angle while pulling the skin against the blade.
\item Flip the fish and repeat. Rinse in cold water, pat dry, and get it onto ice.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bones and what to watch for}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Rib cage}{The only real bone structure. Step 3 removes it.}
\telosfact{The red strip}{Run the knife under the dark lateral muscle and lift it off. On a summer bass this is the difference between a good meal and a bad one.}
\telosfact{Scales}{If you are cooking skin-on, scale it first --- largemouth scales are large, tough and will not soften.}
\telosfact{Check by feel}{Run a finger tip-to-tail before cooking.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{Why bass has a bad name}
Almost every complaint about eating bass comes from one of three things: a fish taken from warm summer water, the dark lateral strip left on, or a fish carried around on a stringer for hours before cleaning. Fix all three --- cold water, trim the strip, ice it immediately --- and it is a genuinely good fish. \sheetref{Handling \& Release}
\end{telospanel}
