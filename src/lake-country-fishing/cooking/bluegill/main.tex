\begin{eatingfacts}
\telosfact{Verdict}{Sweet, delicate and white. Slightly softer than perch but every bit as good, and there are usually a lot more of them. \telosrating{5}}
\telosfact{Texture}{Fine and tender. It will fall apart if you handle it roughly in the pan --- that is the only difficulty.}
\telosfact{Best size}{8\,in and up to fillet. Below that, pan-dress. A 9\,in bluegill is a trophy and a superb meal.}
\telosfact{Yield}{Poor --- about 22\%. The deep body shape means a lot of fish becomes a little fillet. Budget three or four fish per person.}
\telosfact{Ethics}{June bed fish are the easiest to catch and the ones the population needs. Take a meal, not a cooler. \sheetref{Bluegill}}
\telosfact{Freezing}{Freeze in a block of water rather than dry --- delicate flesh burns quickly in a freezer.}
\end{eatingfacts}

\section{Filleting a deep-bodied fish}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cut behind the gill plate}, following the curve of the gill cover down and around, to the backbone.
\item \textbf{Run the blade along the backbone} to the tail. On a deep-bodied fish the backbone sits high --- roughly halfway up --- so aim higher than feels right.
\item \textbf{Take the ribs} with a shallow arc, or cut the whole belly section away.
\item \textbf{Skin it} from the tail end. Bluegill skin releases easily.
\item \textbf{Or pan-dress:} scale it with the back of a knife against the grain, gut it, cut the head off behind the gills, trim the fins. Cook whole. For fish under about 8\,in this is both faster and gives more food.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bones and what to watch for}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Spines}{The dorsal spines are sharp. Trim the whole spiny dorsal off first with kitchen shears --- it makes the rest safer and quicker.}
\telosfact{Rib cage}{Fine and easy to lift out.}
\telosfact{No pin bones}{A bluegill fillet is clean once the ribs are out.}
\telosfact{Pan-dressed}{The whole skeleton lifts out in one piece after cooking. Teach a child to do this and they will eat fish for life.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{The teaching fish}
Bluegill is the right fish to learn knife work on: there are lots of them, they are cheap to ruin, and the anatomy is simple and obvious. Let a teenager fillet six badly before you correct anything. The seventh will be fine, and they will have learnt more than any amount of watching.
\end{telospanel}
