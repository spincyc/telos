\begin{eatingfacts}
\telosfact{Verdict}{The mildest and whitest of the panfish, with a slightly larger flake than bluegill. Many people rate it above every other panfish. \telosrating{5}}
\telosfact{Texture}{Soft, delicate, large-flaked for its size. Wants a gentle hand.}
\telosfact{Best size}{10\,in and up. Crappie are thin fish, so a 10\,in crappie yields less than a 10\,in bass.}
\telosfact{Yield}{About 24\%. Two fillets of roughly 2\,oz from a 10\,in fish.}
\telosfact{Skin}{Thin and tears easily. Skin from the tail with a firm grip.}
\telosfact{Winter fish}{Crappie taken through the ice are the best of the year --- cold water firms the flesh noticeably.}
\end{eatingfacts}

\section{Filleting}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cut down behind the gill plate} and the pectoral fin, angling slightly toward the head, until the blade meets the backbone. Do not cut through it.
\item \textbf{Turn the blade flat} and run it along the top of the backbone to the tail in one long stroke, letting the spine guide the knife. Leave the fillet attached at the tail so it acts as a handle.
\item \textbf{Slice the rib cage out} in a shallow arc, taking as little flesh with it as you can.
\item \textbf{Skin it:} fillet skin-side down, grip the tail, run the knife between skin and flesh at a slight downward angle while pulling the skin against the blade.
\item Flip the fish and repeat. Rinse in cold water, pat dry, and get it onto ice.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bones and what to watch for}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{The mouth}{The membrane is famously thin, which is a fishing problem rather than a cleaning one --- but it also means the head end tears easily under the knife. Cut, do not pull.}
\telosfact{Rib cage}{Fine and easily lifted.}
\telosfact{No pin bones}{Clean fillet once the ribs are out.}
\telosfact{Handle gently}{Crappie flesh is the softest here. Do not stack fillets under weight.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Cooking it}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.18\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{How}\\
\midrule
Lightly fried & Seasoned flour only --- a heavy batter buries a fish this mild. Butter, medium heat, two minutes a side.\\
Broiled with butter & Fillets on a buttered tray, paprika, lemon, six to eight minutes under a hot broiler. Nothing else needed.\\
Fish chowder & Crappie's softness is an asset in a chowder. Potato, onion, bacon, cream, and the fillets added in the last four minutes so they flake through.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{telospanel}{Do not batter it heavily}
Crappie is the mildest thing you will catch in these lakes, and the standard beer batter that suits walleye completely overwhelms it. Flour, butter and lemon. If you cannot taste the fish, the recipe is wrong for it.
\end{telospanel}
