\begin{eatingfacts}
\telosfact{Verdict}{Good --- white, mild and perfectly pleasant, if a little softer than perch or bluegill. Badly under-rated because it is so easy to catch. \telosrating{3}}
\telosfact{Texture}{Softer and coarser than the other panfish. Best cooked hot and fast.}
\telosfact{Freshness matters}{This is the one panfish that noticeably deteriorates on a stringer. Ice it immediately and eat it the same day and it is genuinely good; leave it warm for three hours and it is not.}
\telosfact{Best size}{8\,in and up. Pine Lake's gravel produces plenty.}
\telosfact{Yield}{About 22\%. Deep-bodied, like a bluegill.}
\telosfact{Counts as}{Panfish, in the 25 per day aggregate.}
\end{eatingfacts}

\section{Filleting}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cut down behind the gill plate} and the pectoral fin, angling slightly toward the head, until the blade meets the backbone. Do not cut through it.
\item \textbf{Turn the blade flat} and run it along the top of the backbone to the tail in one long stroke, letting the spine guide the knife. Leave the fillet attached at the tail so it acts as a handle.
\item \textbf{Slice the rib cage out} in a shallow arc, taking as little flesh with it as you can.
\item \textbf{Skin it:} fillet skin-side down, grip the tail, run the knife between skin and flesh at a slight downward angle while pulling the skin against the blade.
\item Flip the fish and repeat. Rinse in cold water, pat dry, and get it onto ice.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bones and what to watch for}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Spines}{Six anal spines --- the identification mark --- and a sharp spiny dorsal. Shear the dorsal off before you start.}
\telosfact{Gill covers}{Sharp-edged. Handle from the front.}
\telosfact{Rib cage}{Fine, lifts out easily.}
\telosfact{Dark strip}{Trim the lateral dark muscle as you would on a bass. It is where any muddiness sits.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Cooking it}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.18\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{How}\\
\midrule
Hot and fast & Cornmeal, very hot oil, ninety seconds a side. The softer flesh needs speed --- a slow pan turns it to mush.\\
In a fish boil & Rock bass hold up in a pot better than they do in a pan. Potatoes, onions, salt, then the fish in for the last five minutes.\\
Cakes & Like bluegill, a good candidate for poaching, flaking and binding into patties when you have caught a lot of them.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{telospanel}{The fish that saves the trip}
Rock bass are aggressive, abundant, easy for a child to catch, and perfectly good on a plate the same evening. On a slow morning with a bored ten-year-old, a rock bass every three casts is worth more than a walleye you did not catch. \sheetref{A First Trip}
\end{telospanel}
