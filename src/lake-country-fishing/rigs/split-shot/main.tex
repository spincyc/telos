\begin{telosfacts}[0.26]
\telosfact{What it does}{One small weight pinched on the line 12--24\,in above a plain hook. It gives just enough weight to cast and to sink, and leaves the bait free to move naturally.}
\telosfact{Why it matters}{Every other rig on these sheets adds hardware, and hardware adds unnatural motion. When fish are wary, less rig catches more fish.}
\telosfact{Components}{A hook. A split shot. That is all.}
\telosfact{Shot size}{BB or size 5 for panfish; size 3 or larger, or two shot, for bass and smallmouth in deeper water.}
\telosfact{Removable shot}{Buy the kind with wings on the back so you can reopen and reposition them. You will move the shot constantly.}
\telosfact{Catches}{Everything. Rock bass, perch, bluegill, smallmouth on a live crayfish, walleye on a leech.}
\end{telosfacts}
\end{minipage}

\section{Fish it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Pinch one shot on the line, using pliers or your teeth --- but never on fluorocarbon leader you care about, because crimping weakens it at that point.
\item Cast out and let it sink on a semi-tight line, watching where the line enters the water.
\item Retrieve in slow, small pulls with long pauses, or simply let it sit on the bottom and wait.
\item Move the shot to change how the bait behaves: closer to the hook makes it sink faster and act tighter, further away lets it drift and swim more freely.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Where it beats a better rig}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Wary fish}{Pine Lake in flat calm. A single small shot is less unnatural than a jig head or a bullet weight.}
\telosfact{Live crayfish}{Hook one through the tail with a plain hook and one shot, and drift it across gravel at Pine spots \textbf{2}, \textbf{3} or \textbf{4}. Very few smallmouth refuse it.}
\telosfact{Teaching}{Two components and no rigging. A child can build it in twenty seconds and understand what every part does.}
\telosfact{Repair}{When you snap off the fancy rig in a rock pile and the light is going, this is the rig you can retie in the dark.}
\telosfact{Not for}{Heavy weed --- the exposed hook fouls. Use a \sheetref{Texas rig} there instead.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{When to use it}
Whenever you catch yourself adding hardware to solve a problem that is actually caused by hardware. Start every unfamiliar spot with a split shot and a piece of live bait: it tells you what lives there before you commit to a technique.
\end{telospanel}
