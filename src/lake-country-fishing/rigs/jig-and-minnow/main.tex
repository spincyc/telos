\begin{telosfacts}[0.26]
\telosfact{What it does}{Weight and hook in one piece, so you feel everything and control the depth absolutely. There is nothing between your hand and the bait.}
\telosfact{Why it matters}{In cold water a walleye's take is a barely perceptible change in weight. A jig transmits that; almost nothing else does.}
\telosfact{Weight}{1/8\,oz to 15 ft, 1/4\,oz to 25 ft, 3/8\,oz deeper or in wind. Use the lightest head that still lets you feel bottom.}
\telosfact{Head shape}{Round for vertical work; a stand-up head for dragging on a clean bottom.}
\telosfact{Bait}{A 2--3\,in fathead or shiner hooked up through both lips. A half crawler or a leech works in warmer water.}
\telosfact{Line}{6--8\,lb. Braid with a fluorocarbon leader if you want maximum feel in deep water.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Fish it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Lower or cast, and let it fall on a \textbf{semi-tight} line. Watch and feel --- a good share of bites happen on the initial drop, and on a fully slack line you will never know.
\item When it hits bottom, lift the rod tip about six inches and \textbf{let it fall back under control}, following it down with the rod. The fall is the presentation. The lift only sets up the next fall.
\item Pause on the bottom for a two-count. Repeat.
\item If you are casting, work the jig back with the same lift-and-fall while the boat holds on the contour.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reading the bite}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{The classic take}{You lift and the jig feels \emph{heavy} --- as though you picked up a wet leaf. That is a walleye. Sweep the rod immediately.}
\telosfact{The tick}{A single sharp tap on the fall. Set at once.}
\telosfact{Nothing}{Sometimes the jig simply stops falling early. Set.}
\telosfact{Do not snap}{A firm upward sweep sets a single sharp hook. A violent snap tears it out of a soft mouth.}
\telosfact{Stay vertical}{Keep the line as close to straight up and down as the drift allows. Line at an angle costs you feel and costs you hooksets.}
\telosfact{Where}{Pine spots \textbf{2}, \textbf{3}, \textbf{4} and \textbf{8}; North spots \textbf{2}, \textbf{3} and \textbf{4}. 15--35 ft.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{When to use it}
Cold water --- ice-out through May, and again from October on --- and any time you can see fish on electronics and want to put something on their heads. It is also the best rig for teaching feel, because everything that happens to the jig arrives in your hand.
\end{telospanel}
