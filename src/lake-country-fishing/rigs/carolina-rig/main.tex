\begin{telosfacts}[0.26]
\telosfact{What it does}{A heavy weight stays on the bottom and telegraphs exactly what the bottom is made of, while the bait trails behind on a long leader, floating free.}
\telosfact{Versus Texas}{A Texas rig puts the weight \emph{at} the bait; a Carolina rig separates them. Texas gets into cover; Carolina covers ground and keeps the bait visible.}
\telosfact{Components}{3/8--3/4\,oz weight, glass or brass bead, barrel swivel, 18--36\,in leader, offset hook, soft plastic.}
\telosfact{The bead}{Sits between weight and swivel. It protects the knot and it clicks --- an audible attractor on a hard bottom.}
\telosfact{Leader length}{Short (18\,in) in cold water or heavy weed; long (36\,in) in clear water and on a clean bottom.}
\telosfact{Line}{15\,lb main, 10--12\,lb leader, so a snag breaks off the bait and not the whole rig.}
\end{telosfacts}
\end{minipage}

\section{Build it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Thread the \textbf{weight} onto the main line, then the \textbf{bead}.
\item Tie the main line to a \textbf{barrel swivel}.
\item Tie the \textbf{leader} to the other end of the swivel, then the \textbf{hook} to the leader.
\item Rig the plastic weedless exactly as for a \sheetref{Texas rig}, or on an open hook if the bottom is clean.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Fish it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Cast long}{This is a long-cast rig. The weight carries it, and the long leader means it needs a slow, wide casting stroke or it will tangle.}
\telosfact{Drag, do not hop}{Sweep the rod sideways, low and slow, to drag the weight across the bottom. Reel up the slack, reposition, sweep again.}
\telosfact{Feel the bottom}{You are mapping structure by touch. Sand feels smooth, gravel grinds, rock knocks, weeds load and release. Slow down when the bottom changes --- that is where fish are.}
\telosfact{The bite}{Usually a distinct tick or a mushy weight. Reel down until tight, then sweep. Do not snap.}
\telosfact{Where}{Pine spot \textbf{5} on the outside weed edge, and the flats around the base of the bar at \textbf{4}. North spots \textbf{3} and \textbf{4}.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{When to use it}
When you need to find fish on a big, featureless-looking flat. The Carolina rig is a search tool that happens to catch fish: it tells you where the bottom changes, and bass live where the bottom changes.
\end{telospanel}
