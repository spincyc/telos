\begin{enumerate}
\item Set the secondary meter to a safe DC-voltage range. Leave the secondary unloaded.
\item Tap the button briefly. Record the peak and sign at \textbf{make}; keep it
pressed only long enough to see the reading return toward zero.
\item Release. Record the peak and sign at \textbf{break}. Repeat three times.
\item Remove the core, change nothing else, and repeat one make/break pair.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Work the ideal ratios first}
\begin{teloscard}{Example}
For \(N_p=60\), \(N_s=180\), and a measured primary pulse of
\(\qty{1.8}{V}\):
\[
V_s=V_p\frac{N_s}{N_p}
=\qty{1.8}{V}\left(\frac{180}{60}\right)=\qty{5.4}{V}.
\]
If an ideal secondary supplies \(\qty{12}{mA}\), power balance predicts
\[
I_p\approx I_s\frac{N_s}{N_p}
=\qty{12}{mA}(3)=\qty{36}{mA}.
\]
Higher voltage comes with lower available current; a transformer does not
create power.
\end{teloscard}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Predict \(I_s=V_s/R_L\). Have the adult check meter jacks and ranges.
\item Attach the resistor only after the open-circuit voltage test passes.
\item Use identical brief taps; record loaded voltage and current. Disconnect.
\item Calculate \(P_s=V_sI_s\). Treat pulse readings as comparisons, not
precision AC power measurements.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teloscard}{Where the ideal model loses}
Loaded voltage falls because copper resistance causes \(I^2R\) heating,
leakage flux misses the other winding, and the core dissipates energy through
hysteresis and eddy currents. Shorted turns are faults, not experiments: they
can draw high current and heat rapidly.
\end{teloscard}

\begin{gate}{Fault-isolation order}
Battery out. Inspect. Check winding continuity. Check isolation to the core.
Verify meter leads and range. Restore only the open-circuit secondary test.
Add the known load last. Never “see whether it clears” by holding the button.
\end{gate}

\section{Final proof: make four claims from evidence}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Changing flux:} show make and break pulses, opposite signs, and
near-zero held reading.
\item \textbf{Magnetic coupling:} compare the same winding pair with and
without the closed core.
\item \textbf{Turns ratio:} compare measured pulse ratios with \(N_s/N_p\)
for at least two winding pairs.
\item \textbf{Non-ideal behavior:} show that a known load reduces secondary
voltage and account for at least two loss mechanisms.
\end{enumerate}
