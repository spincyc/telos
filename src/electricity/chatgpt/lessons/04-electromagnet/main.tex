\def\procedure{
\subsection*{Stage 1: make a repeatable coil}
\buildstrip
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item Wind \(20\) close, single-layer turns around the cardboard tube. Tape the winding so neither the turn count nor winding length can change. Slide the bolt in only after checking that the tube moves freely.
\item Adult checks continuity and measures coil resistance with the battery disconnected: \(R=\rule{16mm}{.4pt}\,\Omega\). If the meter shows open circuit, diagnose before energizing.
\item Connect the fused switched holder. Keep the master switch open except during a timed trial.
\end{enumerate}
\noindent\textbf{Checkpoint question.} Why would comparing a loose 20-turn coil with a compact 40-turn coil change two variables at once?

\subsection*{Stage 2: establish the test method}
\testjig
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item Set the lower bolt pole \(5\,\mathrm{mm}\) above a cup of identical clips. Close the switch for exactly \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\), lift vertically, count the clips still held, then open the switch.
\item Record current during the pulse. Wait at least \(30\,\mathrm{s}\), touch-check for warmth, return every clip to the cup, and repeat three times.
\end{enumerate}

\def\explanation{Current in every turn produces a magnetic field. With the turns wound in the same sense, their axial fields reinforce. The iron core magnetizes and offers a strong path for flux, so it can increase the force at the bolt pole. Reversing current reverses north and south; it does not, by itself, predict a large strength change. Turn count is not the only influence: current, geometry, core, gap, pulse time, and heating can all alter the observation. That is why repeats and controlled variables are part of the physical argument, not paperwork after it.}
\def\extension{Graph mean lift against \(20\), \(30\), and \(40\) turns. Include a vertical uncertainty bar spanning the lowest to highest trial.
