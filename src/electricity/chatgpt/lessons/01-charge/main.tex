\section{The evidence plan}

Static charge is easily lost and easy to imagine. This investigation therefore
uses a control, repeats each comparison, and changes one thing at a time.

\begin{telospanel}{Questions that keep the test honest}
What is held constant: strip length, preparation, suspension, and separation.
What changes: only the pair being compared. What counts as evidence: direction
of first motion, not where the strips finish after touching.
\end{telospanel}

\section{Part A --- establish a baseline}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Cut four tape strips \(15\) cm long. Fold a \(2\) cm handle on each.
    Label two strips \(S_1,S_2\); leave two for the stacked test.
  \item Make two suspension stands: bridge a pencil across a cup and hang a
    strip from its handle. Put the stands \(10\) cm apart.
  \item Suspend \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) without rubbing or peeling them. Bring the
    stands together until the strips are \(2\) cm apart. Do they move before
    air currents settle? \(\bigcirc\) yes \(\bigcirc\) no
  \item Hold a hand \(10\) cm away, then withdraw it. If the strips swing, wait
    until they stop. This is the motion floor for later comparisons.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Part B --- same preparation, then opposite histories}

\subsection{B1. Two strips prepared alike}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Press \(S_1\) flat on the same clean table area ten times with a finger.
    Pull it up briskly by its handle. Touch only the folded handle.
  \item Repeat exactly with \(S_2\). Suspend both immediately.
  \item Starting at \(8\) cm, reduce the gap by \(1\) cm every three seconds.
    At what gap does the first repeatable motion appear? Record its direction.
  \item Discharge, prepare again, and perform three trials. Reverse the
    left/right stands on trial 3. Does the direction follow the strips?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{B2. Separate a stacked pair}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Put one fresh strip on the table and label its handle \(B\) (bottom).
    Press a second strip directly on top and label it \(T\) (top).
  \item Lift the pair together by \(B\). Pause: before separation, predict the
    sign relationship between \(T\) and \(B\): \rule{.12\linewidth}{.3pt}
  \item Pull \(T\) and \(B\) apart in one smooth motion. Suspend them without
    letting them touch your clothes, hair, or each other.
  \item Compare \(T\) with \(T\) from a repeated pair, \(B\) with \(B\), then
    \(T\) with \(B\). Reset between comparisons.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Part C --- a charged object and a neutral can}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Lay the empty can on a level table. Mark its starting line. Confirm
    that it remains still for ten seconds with the comb \(30\) cm away.
  \item Rub the comb with wool exactly ten strokes. Keep the same hand, stroke
    length, and comb face for every trial.
  \item Hold the comb parallel to the can, \(10\) cm from its nearest surface.
    Do not touch. Move inward in \(1\) cm steps, pausing three seconds.
  \item Record the first distance at which the can begins rolling toward the
    comb. Stop the comb; does the can continue? Move it sideways; does the can
    track it?
  \item Return the can to its line and repeat three times. Recharge the comb
    with ten strokes before every trial.
  \item Control: repeat the distance sequence after touching the comb to your
    hand and table. A smaller or absent response supports the charge claim.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Build the proof}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Repulsion test:} Two independently prepared alike strips
    repelled in \rule{1cm}{.3pt} of three trials. Repulsion rules out a merely
    neutral partner and is evidence that the strips carried like signs.
  \item \textbf{Opposite-history test:} \(T\)-\(T\) and \(B\)-\(B\) showed
    \rule{2.1cm}{.3pt}; \(T\)-\(B\) showed \rule{2.1cm}{.3pt}. The consistent
    pattern supports two charge signs.
  \item \textbf{Conservation claim:} Separating the joined system redistributes
    charge. The evidence does \emph{not} show charge appearing from nothing:
    opposite changes occur on the two members of the original pair.
  \item \textbf{Polarization test:} The charged comb moved the neutral can from
    a median threshold of \rule{1cm}{.3pt} cm, while the discharged control
    \rule{2.5cm}{.3pt}. Because the can began neutral, attraction alone does not
    establish opposite net charge; charge shifted within it.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{telospanel}{Verification questions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Which single observation best distinguishes like charge from
    polarization? Why?
  \item What result followed the object when the supports were swapped?
  \item Does a smaller threshold distance mean a stronger or weaker observed
    interaction under this procedure?
  \item What uncontrolled variable most limits your claim?
  \item Write one sentence that separates observation from explanation:
    ``I observed \rule{.28\linewidth}{.3pt}; this supports
    \rule{.28\linewidth}{.3pt} because \rule{.23\linewidth}{.3pt}.''
\end{enumerate}
\end{telospanel}

\section{Explain from the evidence}

Like charge signs repel and unlike signs attract. Peeling and rubbing transfer
charge between materials; for the combined system, charge is conserved. A
charged object can attract a neutral conductor because mobile charge in the
conductor redistributes. The nearer opposite-sign region experiences the
stronger interaction. Humidity and contaminated surfaces provide leakage paths,
so null results belong in the record.

\section{Change one thing}

Repeat the can trial at two measured humidity levels, or compare two insulating
materials. Keep geometry, stroke count, timing, and observer fixed. Plot the
three threshold distances rather than reporting only the strongest trial.
