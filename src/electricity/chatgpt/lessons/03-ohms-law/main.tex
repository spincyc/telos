\rolecard

\begin{materials}
\item Switched holder for 2 AA cells
\item 100, 220, and 470 \(\Omega\) resistors
\item Digital multimeter with fused current input
\item Four insulated clip leads
\item Graph paper and pencil
\item Optional second meter for simultaneous readings
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
For the same source, rank the three resistors from greatest to least current.
Then predict whether \(V/I\) will agree with the marked resistance within the
combined tolerances.
\end{predict}

\begin{teloshazard}{Meter state is part of the circuit}
An ammeter is a low-resistance path. Never place it directly across a cell or
battery holder. Move the red lead back to the voltage/resistance jack
immediately after every current measurement. Change connections only with the
holder switched off and disconnected. If a meter, lead, cell, or resistor
warms, disconnect at once; do not repeat until the cause is found.
\end{teloshazard}

\section{Questions before touching a lead}

\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which meter jack is fused?
\item What is the fuse rating printed on the meter?
\item Does the selected current range exceed the predicted current?
\item Which measurements require an energized circuit?
\item Which require an isolated, unpowered component?
\item Why does resistance mode supply its own small test current?
\item Where must a voltmeter connect: in series or in parallel?
\item Where must an ammeter connect?
\item What reading would ``OL'' mean on each selected function?
\item What single action ends every abnormal test?
\end{enumerate}
\end{multicols}

\section{Controlled verification}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Measure the open-circuit source voltage \(V_s\). Record meter function,
range, jacks, resolution, and reading.
\item For each resistor, perform State 1 and record \(R_\text{meter}\).
\item Build State 2. Close briefly; record loaded \(V_R\). Open and disconnect.
\item Reconfigure to State 3. Close briefly; record \(I\). Open and disconnect.
\item Repeat each energized reading once after rebuilding the connection.
Do not average away a disagreement; investigate it.
\item Calculate \(R_\text{calc}=V_R/I\) and \(P=V_RI\).
\item Plot \(V_R\) vertically against \(I\) horizontally. A fitted slope is an
estimate of resistance; do not force the line through zero without justification.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked calculation}

Suppose \(V_R=2.91\ \mathrm{V}\) and \(I=13.1\ \mathrm{mA}
=0.0131\ \mathrm{A}\):
\[
R_\text{calc}=\frac{V_R}{I}
=\frac{2.91\ \mathrm{V}}{0.0131\ \mathrm{A}}
=222\ \Omega ,
\qquad
P=V_RI=(2.91)(0.0131)=0.0381\ \mathrm{W}.
\]
For a 220 \(\Omega\), 5\% resistor, the marked interval is
\(220(1\pm0.05)=209\) to \(231\ \Omega\). The calculated value lies inside it,
but that agreement is not yet proof: meter uncertainty and repeatability also
belong in the comparison.

\section{Uncertainty without false precision}

Use the accuracy statement from the meter manual. If it says
\(\pm(a\%\text{ of reading}+b\text{ digits})\), then
\[
u_x=(a/100)|x|+b(\text{least significant digit}).
\]
Example: on a display reading \(2.91\ \mathrm{V}\), with accuracy
\(\pm(0.5\%+2\text{ digits})\) and resolution \(0.01\ \mathrm{V}\),
\[
u_V=0.005(2.91)+2(0.01)=0.0346\ \mathrm{V},
\quad V=2.91\pm0.035\ \mathrm{V}.
\]
A conservative bound for division is
\[
\frac{u_R}{R}\leq\frac{u_V}{|V|}+\frac{u_I}{|I|}.
\]
Record the bound as an interval. Round the reported result to the same decimal
place as its uncertainty.

\section{Challenge questions}

\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Why is \(V/I\) a stronger test than current alone?
\item Why measure loaded source voltage?
\item What does the slope of the \(V\)-versus-\(I\) graph mean?
\item Which axis assignment makes the slope equal \(R\)?
\item Does a straight line prove the resistor is ohmic at every temperature?
\item How can lead resistance matter most for the smallest resistor?
\item Why might resistance mode and \(V/I\) disagree slightly?
\item How does ammeter burden voltage alter the circuit?
\item Why can a continuity tone coexist with a bad loaded connection?
\item When is ``OL'' evidence of an open path, and when is it only over-range?
\item Which digits in your calculation are not supported by the meters?
\item What observation would make you reject rather than average a run?
\item Does overlap of intervals prove the marked value is correct?
\item What additional independent check would strengthen the claim?
\end{enumerate}
\end{multicols}

\section{Controlled extension}

Add a second resistor in series, then in parallel. Before connecting, predict
equivalent resistance and maximum current. Accept the new circuit only after
repeating States 0--3, the uncertainty comparison, and the acceptance proof.
An LED is not the acceptance standard for Ohm's law: it is nonlinear and
polarity-sensitive.
