\def\procedure{\small
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Find the natural timing.} Pull the mass aside by \(\qty{3}{cm}\); release without pushing. Time ten complete cycles from one same-direction crossing to the eleventh. Repeat three times. Calculate
\[
T=\frac{t_{10}}{10},\qquad f_0=\frac{1}{T}.
\]
Record all trials rather than choosing the neatest one. If the three periods differ by more than \(5\%\), inspect the pivot, release, and cycle count before continuing.

\item \textbf{Make push size repeatable.} Put a tape index beside the lowest point. A partner gives one light sideways tap only as the mass crosses that index in the chosen direction. Rehearse with the pendulum still: same finger, same travel, no following-through.

\item \textbf{Sweep the drive timing.} Reset to the same \(\qty{3}{cm}\) release before each run. Give ten equal taps at intervals
\[
0.70T,\quad 0.85T,\quad 1.00T,\quad 1.15T,\quad 1.30T.
\]
After tap 10, read maximum displacement against the ruler. Stop a run if the mass or string approaches the stand.
\end{enumerate}

\def\explanation{\small
\textbf{Decide from the curve.} A resonance claim needs more than ``it got large.'' The response must rise toward one drive interval and fall away on both sides, with the peak near the independently measured \(T\). The taps supply energy; phase determines whether each tap adds to or opposes the motion. Resonance selects efficient transfer. It creates no energy.

\midrule
All points alike & Tap timing or tap strength was not controlled; widen the timing sweep.\\
Peak shifted & Recalculate \(T\); check whether the string length or mass position changed.\\
Jagged left/right points & More trials are needed; the ruler reading or phase call is inconsistent.\\
Amplitude grows without bound & Stop: geometry changed, the stand is moving, or the safe-motion limit was missed.\\
Vane peak lower and wider & Expected damping signature: more energy lost each cycle and poorer frequency selectivity.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\medskip
For an ideal electrical resonator,
\[
f_0=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}.
\]
Capacitor electric-field energy and inductor magnetic-field energy trade back and forth; resistance dissipates part of that energy on every cycle. This is an analogy supported by the shared timing, response-peak, and damping patterns—not proof that a pendulum contains an electrical circuit.}
\def\extension{\small
\textbf{Final proof.} On one graph, plot amplitude against \(t_{\rm tap}/T\) for the bare pendulum and vane. Mark \(1.00\). Then answer:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does each curve have an interior maximum rather than merely increasing from left to right?
\item Is the bare peak within one tested timing step of \(1.00T\)?
\item Does the vane shorten the half-amplitude decay time and reduce or broaden the peak?
\item Which observation rules out ``all repeated pushes work equally well''?
\item What measurement uncertainty could move your conclusion?
\end{enumerate}
Accept the resonance claim only if the independent period, two-sided response peak, and damping change agree. Otherwise report ``not yet demonstrated'' and name the next controlled repeat.}
