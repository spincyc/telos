\subsection{2. Time one baseline trial}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Put a paper direction flag behind the apparatus. Adult clears loose metal and starts the timer, then lowers the wire. Call ``contact'' at \(0\) s; learner counts full turns and calls clockwise or counterclockwise from above.
\item Adult lifts the wire at \(5\) s or sooner for stall, odor, pain, sparking, wobble, or unexpected motion. Record start delay, direction, and turns in five seconds; do not extrapolate a speed from a partial turn.
\item With power open, adult compares wire, cell, and magnet with the room-temperature reference using the back of a finger. If any is distinctly warm, damaged, or uncomfortable, stop for the day. Otherwise cool at least \(60\) s before another trial.
\end{enumerate}

\def\explanation{Current crossing the magnet's field experiences force; the opposed wire arms turn that force into torque about the cell axis. Reversing current or magnetic field reverses the force and rotation. Reversing both preserves the direction. Contact quality changes whether torque beats friction, but it does not change the reversal rule. This apparatus is nearly a direct short, so much of the input can become heat. That is why every energization is timed, adult-controlled, AA alkaline only, never lithium, and separated by an open-circuit thermal check.}
\def\extension{\textbf{Final proof:} Use the smallest safe set of trials to support
the claim ``one reversal reverses motion; two reversals restore it.'' Name the
two trials compared, report direction from above and turns in five seconds,
then identify one result that would have falsified the claim. Finish only when
the adult confirms the circuit is open, all parts are cool and undamaged, the
single magnet is counted into storage, and no wire remains touching the cell.}
