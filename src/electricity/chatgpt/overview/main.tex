\section{What this course is trying to prove}
The course does not ask a learner to accept a named effect because a diagram
looks persuasive. Each lesson asks a question, changes one variable, captures
an observation or measurement, and decides whether the evidence supports the
prediction. A successful apparatus is evidence only when the record says what
was held constant and what would count against the claim.

\begin{predict}
\textbf{Opening question.} Which is stronger evidence: ``the coil worked,'' a
meter reading of \qty{0.42}{V}, or a table showing that the reading changed
when distance changed? What extra information would make your choice
repeatable by another learner?
\end{predict}

2 & Fields & Direction and magnitude of test-object deflection at five or more
locations. & Do arrows describe a positive test charge, rather than visible
threads in space? & Annotated field map.\\
3 & Ohm's law & Matched voltage and current pairs with one resistor held
constant. & Does $V/I$ remain approximately stable within measurement
resolution? & Data table and graph.\\
4 & Electromagnet & Pickup or deflection versus current or turn count, changing
only one. & Can the learner distinguish current, turns, and core material? &
Controlled-comparison table.\\
5 & Induction & Meter direction and magnitude while motion direction or speed
changes. & Is a changing magnetic situation required? & Motion/response
sequence.\\
6 & Transformers & Primary and secondary turns with measured voltage ratio. &
Does the ratio predict direction and approximate size of change? & Ratio
calculation with uncertainty.\\
7 & Capacitors & Charge/discharge voltage at fixed time intervals using only
the bounded low-voltage setup. & Can stored energy be distinguished from a
source still connected? & Time series and safe-discharge check.\\
8 & Resonance & Response across a controlled frequency sweep in the supplied
low-energy arrangement. & Is the peak repeatable and broader than reading
noise? & Sweep plot with peak band.\\
9 & Coupling & Received signal versus coil distance or orientation. & Does one
geometric change predictably alter transfer? & Geometry sketch plus readings.\\
10 & Spark gap & Explanatory comparison of historical discharge apparatus;
no learner construction or energization. & Can the learner explain why the
course boundary excludes exposed high voltage? & Source-based mechanism map.\\
\bottomrule

\section{Reading the result}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Support.} The repeated observations change in the predicted direction,
and the effect is larger than the reading resolution or ordinary scatter.

\textbf{Refine.} The direction agrees but the size does not. Check idealized
assumptions, meter loading, resistance, geometry, timing, and uncertainty.

\textbf{Reject for now.} The pattern is absent or reverses. Preserve the result;
check the setup and prediction without quietly deleting inconvenient trials.

\textbf{Inconclusive.} The effect is comparable to noise, a control changed, or
the apparatus could not be inspected. Improve the test before making a claim.
\end{multicols}

\begin{gate}{Diagnostic pause}
If two trials disagree, do not add energy or exceed the boundary. Disconnect.
Check the drawing against the setup; confirm meter mode and range; inspect each
connection; repeat a known baseline; then alter only the planned variable.
\end{gate}
\begin{predict}
\textbf{Learner questions.} What result would change your mind? Which control is
most likely to drift? What is the smallest change your instrument can show?
Could another mechanism produce the same observation? What should a second
person be able to reproduce from your record alone?
\end{predict}
\section{The bound notebook}
Number every page. Never remove a failed trial. Begin each lesson on a new
spread and give every sketch, table, and graph a stable identifier such as
\texttt{L04-F2}. Cross-reference revisions rather than erasing the earlier
model. Paste or transcribe meter specifications and part values beside the
first use.
