\section{Make the detector}
Wind 200--300 turns of fine enamelled wire around a cardboard tube; tape the
coil, scrape the two ends, and connect it to a digital multimeter on its most
sensitive DC voltage range. The coil has no battery.

\section{Build the compact model}
A \emph{change} in magnetic flux through the coil induces an emf. Reversing
motion or magnet orientation reverses the sign. Faster change generally makes
a larger magnitude. The induced effect opposes the change that produces it:
this is the qualitative Faraday--Lenz model.

\begin{telospanel}{Where the energy comes from}
The meter response is not free energy. Your hand does mechanical work against
the induced interaction; some energy transfers electrically and ultimately
becomes thermal energy. A generator scales this conversion with controlled
motion, magnetic fields and circuits.
\end{telospanel}

\section{A fairer extension}
Compare 100 and 300 turns using the same magnet, tube, travel distance and a
metronome to time the stroke. Make five trials per coil and compare medians.
Then name the largest uncontrolled variable. Do not compare hand speed by
memory.

\begin{teloscard}{What this does—and does not—show}
It supports a relationship among changing flux, polarity and induced emf. It
does not establish the waveform of grid AC, transformer safety, or permission
to connect a coil to household power.
\end{teloscard}
