\section{Lab A — What does “complete path” mean?}
\textbf{Build:} two AA cells, a 330 \(\Omega\) resistor and an LED in series.
Try each change with power disconnected: reverse LED; remove one connection;
bypass the resistor \textbf{only as a prediction—do not connect it}.

\section{Lab B — Make an \(I\)-\(V\) evidence table}
Use \textbf{one} 220 \(\Omega\), 0.25 W resistor. Measure resistance with the
battery disconnected. Then use 1, 2, 3 and 4 AA cells and measure voltage
\emph{across} the resistor. Measure current only after the adult verifies that
the red lead is in the correct current jack and the meter is inserted in series.
Connect for at most five seconds per reading.

\begin{gate}{Power rating}
Before the four-cell trial, calculate \(P=V^2/R\) using the measured resistance
and expected battery voltage. Proceed only if the result is below 0.25 W.
\end{gate}

\section{Lab C — Series and parallel are connection claims}
Using two 1 k\(\Omega\) resistors, predict and then measure equivalent resistance
with \textbf{all batteries removed}. Series should exceed either resistor;
parallel should be less than either. Explain the result as available paths, then
check with \(R_s=R_1+R_2\) and \(1/R_p=1/R_1+1/R_2\).

\section{Read the graph, not just the formula}
Plot \(I\) vertically and \(V\) horizontally. If the points lie near a straight
line through zero, \(V=IR\) is a useful model over this range. A slope changing
with temperature would mean “constant \(R\)” failed—not that Ohm's law is a
magic rule every component must obey.

\begin{telospanel}{Units tell a story}
\(1\,{\rm A}=1\,{\rm C/s}\), \(1\,{\rm V}=1\,{\rm W/A}\), and
\(1\,\Omega=1\,{\rm V/A}\). Power \(P=VI\) is an energy-transfer rate.
\end{telospanel}
