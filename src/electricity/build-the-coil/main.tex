\midrule

  An older non-GFCI unit. A modern electronic sign transformer will not survive
  this duty. \textbf{This is the lethal component.}\\
Tank capacitor & \qtyrange{5}{10}{nF} at \qty{20}{kV}+, as a series-parallel
  string of pulse-rated film capacitors (an ``MMC'') &
  Never electrolytic, never ordinary film. Each capacitor gets its own
  \qty{10}{M\Omega} bleeder resistor across it.\\
Main gap & Two \qtyrange{1}{1.5}{in} copper pipe electrodes, adjustable, or a
  multi-gap string & Round and smooth, mounted so the spacing can be set
  repeatably. Fan-cooled if you run it more than briefly.\\
Safety gap & Fixed, set slightly wider than the main gap & Protects the
  transformer from reflected spikes. Not optional.\\
Primary & 10--15 turns of \qty{1/4}{in} copper tube or heavy wire, flat spiral,
  \emph{tapped} & The tap is the tuning control. Leave it accessible.\\
Secondary & \qtyrange{900}{1200}{} turns of 24--26 AWG enamelled wire on
  \qtyrange{3}{4}{in} PVC or acrylic, aspect ratio about 4:1 or 5:1 &
  Close-wound, no gaps, no overlaps. Sealed with polyurethane afterwards.\\
Topload & Aluminium dryer duct formed into a torus, or a spun ball &
  Smooth. Every sharp point is a place the coil will arc from prematurely.\\
Ground & Its own rod, driven outside & \textbf{Never} the mains earth.\\
\bottomrule

\section{Build order --- and who does what}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{1. Grounding stick}{Build this \emph{first}, before any high-voltage
  part exists. Hardwood or PVC handle, metal hook, heavy braid to the ground rod.
  Nothing else gets built until it is finished and hanging on the wall.}
\telosfact{2. Secondary}{The long job and the best one to share. Wind slowly and
  evenly, keeping tension constant; a drill on its slowest speed with the form
  chucked helps. Count in hundreds and mark the form. Two coats of polyurethane
  when done. \emph{Boys do this.}}
\telosfact{3. Primary}{Flat spiral on an insulating cross, turns evenly spaced,
  with a clip lead for the tap. \emph{Boys do this.}}
\telosfact{4. Topload}{Form the duct into a ring, tape and smooth it, mount it
  on a plate above the secondary. \emph{Boys do this.}}
\telosfact{5. Capacitor bank}{Series-parallel string on a board, every capacitor
  with its bleeder resistor. \emph{Boys can assemble it; adult checks every
  joint.}}
\telosfact{6. Gaps, enclosure, wiring}{Main gap, safety gap, interlocked
  enclosure, switched and fused mains inlet, indicator lamp.
  \emph{Adult only.}}
\telosfact{7. Ground rod}{Driven outside, heavy strap back to the secondary base
  by the shortest route you can manage.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Tuning it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Calculate first.} Work out the secondary's resonant frequency from
  its dimensions, and set the primary tap to the turn that gives the primary
  tank the same $f_0$. Lesson 8 is the whole of this step.
\item \textbf{Start with the gap narrow} and the tap near your calculated turn.
\item \textbf{Fire it in short bursts.} Seconds, not minutes. Unplug, short the
  capacitor with the grounding stick, move the tap one turn, reconnect, fire
  again. \textbf{Every single adjustment goes through that sequence.}
\item \textbf{Watch the streamers.} Longer and brighter means closer to tune.
  Work outward from the calculated turn in both directions and keep a written
  log of tap position against streamer length --- you will not remember.
\item \textbf{Then the gap.} Once the tap is right, widen the gap a little at a
  time. Wider means a higher firing voltage and more energy per shot, up to the
  point where the transformer can no longer charge the capacitor fast enough and
  the output falls off again.
\item \textbf{Stop when it stops improving.} A small coil producing a clean
  6--12\,in streamer is a complete success and there is nothing to prove by
  pushing it further.
\end{enumerate}

\section{If it does not work}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.28\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Symptom} & \textbf{Look at}\\
\midrule
No spark at the gap at all & Transformer not energized; interlock open; gap far
  too wide; capacitor open circuit.\\
Gap fires but no output & Tuning is a long way off, or the ground connection is
  poor or missing. Check the ground first --- it is the most common fault.\\
Weak, thin streamers & Off tune. Move the tap. Also check for a cracked
  capacitor or a sooty gap.\\
Safety gap firing constantly & The main gap is set too wide, so voltage is
  reaching the safety gap first. Narrow the main gap.\\
Arcs from the secondary to the primary & Coupling too tight, or the topload is
  too small. Raise the secondary relative to the primary; add a ``strike ring''.\\
Internal arcing in the secondary & Coupling much too tight, or turns damaged.
  This ruins a secondary. Stop and inspect.\\
Transformer getting hot & Running too long. Short bursts only.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{teloshazard}{Before every run, read the checklist aloud}
Grounding stick in reach. Capacitor shorted, hands out. Enclosure closed,
interlock tested. RF ground connected and tight. Everyone with an implant out of
the room and all electronics elsewhere. Second person present who knows where the
plug is. Safety glasses and hearing protection on. Extinguisher in reach, bench
clear. Everyone beyond arm's length of the longest streamer. The named adult is
the one closing the switch. The full list, and why each item is on it, is on the
\textbf{Safety} sheet --- and it belongs on the wall, not in a drawer.
\end{teloshazard}

\begin{telospanel}{What you built}
A transformer charges a capacitor. A gap of air, switching in under a
microsecond, dumps that capacitor into a tuned coil. That coil rings, and over
many cycles hands its energy to a second coil tuned to the same note, loosely
enough that the voltage has time to build. The result leaves the topload as a
streamer, because the electric field at its surface has grown strong enough to
tear air apart. Every clause of that is a lesson you did with a battery and a
meter first --- which is the only reason any of it makes sense now.
\end{telospanel}
