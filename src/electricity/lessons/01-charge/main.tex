\telosmasthead[Build an electroscope]
\lessonhead{1}{about 90 minutes}{everyone builds; no electrical hazard}

\begin{materials}
\item Sticky tape (regular, not invisible)
\item Two balloons, string
\item A glass jar with a plastic or cork lid
\item Stiff copper wire, about 20\,cm
\item Aluminium foil
\item A plastic comb or PVC pipe offcut
\item A wool sweater or a silk scarf
\item An empty aluminium can
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
Rub a balloon on your hair and hold it near a thin stream of water from a tap.
Does the stream bend \emph{toward} the balloon, \emph{away} from it, or not at
all? Write down which, and one sentence saying why. Do this before you touch the
tap.
\end{predict}

\lessonsection{Do it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two kinds.} Stick two 15\,cm strips of tape onto the bench, then
  pull both off quickly and hang them near each other. They push apart. Now
  stick one strip \emph{on top of} another, pull the pair off together, then
  peel them apart --- now they pull together. Two strips pulled the same way
  repel; two pulled apart from each other attract. There are exactly two kinds
  of charge, and that is an experimental result, not a definition.
\item \textbf{Test the water.} Run the tap to a thin stream. Charge a balloon on
  hair or wool and bring it close. Compare with your prediction.
\item \textbf{Roll the can.} Lay the empty can on its side on a flat table. Bring
  the charged balloon near without touching. The can follows it.
\item \textbf{Build the electroscope.} Push the copper wire through the lid.
  Bend the inside end into a small hook and hang two strips of foil
  (about $1\times4$\,cm) over it so they hang flat against each other. Crumple a
  ball of foil onto the outside end. Seal the lid onto the jar.
\item \textbf{Use it.} Charge the comb on wool and touch it to the foil ball. The
  leaves fly apart and \emph{stay} apart. Touch the ball with your finger: they
  collapse.
\item \textbf{Charge without touching.} Recharge the comb. Bring it \emph{near}
  the ball without contact --- the leaves separate. Take it away --- they fall.
  Now bring it near again, and while it is near, touch the ball with a finger
  from your other hand. Remove your finger first, then the comb. The leaves stay
  apart, and the electroscope now holds the \emph{opposite} charge to the comb.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{What it means}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Two signs}{Charge comes in two kinds, arbitrarily named positive and
  negative. Like repels like; opposite attracts. Everything else in this course
  is a consequence.}
\telosfact{Rubbing moves, it does not create}{Wool gives up electrons and the
  balloon takes them. The wool is left as positive as the balloon is negative.
  Nothing was made; something was moved. That is conservation of charge, and it
  has no known exceptions.}
\telosfact{Why neutral things are attracted}{The water and the can are neutral,
  and they still move. A nearby charge pulls their own charges slightly apart
  --- the near side becomes opposite, the far side like. The near side is closer,
  so attraction wins. This is \textbf{induction}, and it is why the last step
  works.}
\telosfact{The leaves}{Both leaves get the same charge, so they repel. The
  angle is a crude voltmeter: it measures how much charge is on the ball.}
\telosfact{Conductor and insulator}{Your finger drained the charge to earth
  through your body; the glass jar does not. That difference is the whole reason
  wires and insulation both exist.}
\end{telosfacts}

\lessonsection{Break it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Humidity}{Try this on a humid day and again on a dry one. Water in
  the air conducts, and charge leaks away. If nothing works, that is why --- and
  it is also why the Tesla coil will perform differently on different days.}
\telosfact{Longer leaves}{Make one leaf twice as long. Does it move further?
  Why might that be about weight rather than charge?}
\telosfact{Reverse it}{Charge the comb with silk instead of wool. Does the
  electroscope respond the same way? Is the comb's charge the same sign?}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{The link to the coil}
The topload of a Tesla coil is a large smooth conductor that holds charge, and
a streamer is that charge leaving it into the air. The electroscope you just
built is the same physics at a voltage a thousand times smaller. When the coil
runs, you are watching your foil leaves fly apart --- violently, sixty times a
second.
\end{telospanel}
