\telosmasthead[Build a Leyden jar, then measure a time constant]
\lessonhead{7}{about 90 minutes}{everyone builds; battery power only}

\begin{materials}
\item Capacitors: 100\,$\mu$F, 470\,$\mu$F, 1000\,$\mu$F electrolytic
\item Resistors: 1\,k$\Omega$, 10\,k$\Omega$, 100\,k$\Omega$
\item LED, breadboard, 9\,V battery, switch
\item Multimeter, stopwatch
\item A plastic film canister or small jar, aluminium foil, tape
\item The electroscope from Lesson 1
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
A 1000\,$\mu$F capacitor is charged from a 9\,V battery through a 10\,k$\Omega$
resistor. How long until it reaches about two-thirds of 9\,V? Then: you
disconnect the battery entirely and short the capacitor's legs with a
screwdriver a minute later. Does anything happen?
\end{predict}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Charge and watch.} Build the circuit with 1000\,$\mu$F and
  10\,k$\Omega$. Close the switch and read the voltage every two seconds. It
  rises fast, then slower, and creeps toward 9\,V without ever quite arriving.
\item \textbf{Discharge.} Open the switch and connect a resistor across the
  capacitor. Watch it fall the same way, mirrored.
\item \textbf{Change one thing.} Repeat with 100\,k$\Omega$, then back to
  10\,k$\Omega$ but with 100\,$\mu$F. Time how long each takes to reach about
  two-thirds. Multiply $R$ by $C$ each time and compare.
\item \textbf{Store enough to see.} Charge 1000\,$\mu$F to 9\,V, disconnect the
  battery, then connect an LED across it. It glows and fades. The battery is
  gone --- that light is coming out of the capacitor.
\item \textbf{Build a Leyden jar.} Wrap the outside of a film canister in foil
  and line the inside with foil, keeping the two layers from touching. Push a
  wire through the lid to the inner foil. Charge it from a comb rubbed on wool,
  many strokes, holding the outer foil. Then bring the wire near the outer foil
  --- with a \emph{dry stick}, not your fingers --- and watch the spark.
\item \textbf{Feel the difference.} A 1000\,$\mu$F capacitor at 9\,V is
  harmless. The Leyden jar holds far less charge at a far higher voltage and it
  will make a spark and sting. Same energy scale, entirely different behaviour.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{What it means}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{What a capacitor is}{Two conductors separated by an insulator.
  Charge piles up on one plate and is repelled from the other, and the energy
  is stored in the electric field \emph{between} them --- the field you made
  visible in Lesson 2.}
\telosfact{Capacitance}{$Q = CV$. Bigger plates and a thinner gap store more
  charge for the same voltage.}
\telosfact{The time constant}{$\tau = RC$. In one time constant the capacitor
  charges to about 63\% of the way, and after five it is essentially there. Your
  three timings should have scaled exactly with the product $R \times C$.}
\telosfact{Stored energy}{$E = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^2$. Note the \emph{square}: doubling
  the voltage quadruples the stored energy. This is the single most important
  equation in this course, and Lesson 11 is built on it.}
\telosfact{A capacitor is not a battery}{It stores far less energy, but it can
  release all of it in microseconds. A battery is a reservoir; a capacitor is a
  bucket you can tip over instantly. A Tesla coil needs the tipping.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{teloshazard}{The habit that matters later}
The Leyden jar holds its charge after you stop rubbing, and a capacitor bank in
Lesson 11 holds a lethal charge after the power is off. Practise the habit here,
where the worst case is a sting: \textbf{before you touch a capacitor, short it
out with something you are holding by an insulated handle.} Then short it again.
Large capacitors can partially recharge themselves through dielectric absorption
after being discharged once. This is exactly why the grounding stick exists.
\end{teloshazard}

\begin{telospanel}{The link to the coil}
The tank capacitor is the shot the coil fires. The transformer charges it over
several thousandths of a second; the spark gap dumps it in a few millionths.
$E = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^2$ says how much energy is in that shot, and because power
is energy divided by time, dumping a modest amount of energy in a very short
time produces an enormous power --- which is the whole trick.
\end{telospanel}
