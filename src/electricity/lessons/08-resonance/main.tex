\telosmasthead[Swings, wine glasses and the LC tank]
\lessonhead{8}{about 90 minutes}{everyone builds; battery power only}

\begin{materials}
\item String and a weight, for a pendulum
\item An inductor: the 100-turn coil from Lesson 4, or a 1\,mH choke
\item Capacitors: 0.01\,$\mu$F, 0.1\,$\mu$F, 1\,$\mu$F (film, not electrolytic)
\item A signal generator, or a phone app with a tone generator and an amplifier
\item An oscilloscope if you have one; otherwise a multimeter on AC volts
\item A wine glass and water
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
You push a child on a swing. Why does pushing at exactly the right moment work
so much better than pushing hard at random moments? Write the answer in your own
words. Then: an LC circuit rings at one frequency. If you \emph{quadruple} the
capacitance, does the frequency double, halve, quarter, or something else?
\end{predict}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The pendulum.} Hang a weight on a string. Push it at random ---
  it goes nowhere. Push it once each time it swings back to you --- it climbs
  quickly. Now shorten the string by half and find the new rhythm. Every
  oscillator has one frequency at which small, well-timed pushes accumulate.
\item \textbf{The wine glass.} Wet a finger and run it round the rim until it
  sings. Add water and do it again --- the note drops. You changed the effective
  mass of the oscillator, and the frequency followed.
\item \textbf{Build the tank.} Connect your coil and a 0.1\,$\mu$F capacitor in
  parallel. Drive it from the signal generator through a 1\,k$\Omega$ resistor
  and measure the AC voltage across the tank.
\item \textbf{Sweep it.} Step the frequency up in stages, recording the voltage
  each time. Plot voltage against frequency. There is one frequency where the
  reading peaks sharply --- that is $f_0$.
\item \textbf{Change one thing.} Swap to 0.01\,$\mu$F and find the peak again.
  Then 1\,$\mu$F. Compare with your prediction: a hundred-fold change in $C$
  should move $f_0$ by a factor of ten, because the formula has a square root
  in it.
\item \textbf{Check the formula.} Compute $1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$ for each and
  compare with what you measured.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{What it means}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Energy sloshes}{Charge the capacitor and let it go: it drives current
  through the coil, which builds a magnetic field. When the capacitor is empty
  the field collapses, and by Lesson 5 that collapse drives current onward,
  recharging the capacitor the other way round. Back and forth --- the exact
  electrical analogue of the pendulum.}
\telosfact{The resonant frequency}{$f_0 = 1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$. It depends only on
  the coil and the capacitor, not on how hard you hit it.}
\telosfact{Why the peak is sharp}{At $f_0$ small pushes arrive in step and add
  up, so the voltage in the tank climbs far above the driving voltage. Off
  resonance they arrive out of step and cancel.}
\telosfact{Q}{How sharp that peak is, is called the Q of the circuit. Low
  resistance means high Q, means a taller and narrower peak, means more energy
  accumulated before losses eat it. A Tesla coil secondary wants the highest Q
  you can build.}
\telosfact{Damping}{Add a resistor in series and sweep again. The peak flattens.
  Resistance is what stops the swing.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{The link to the coil}
Both halves of a Tesla coil are LC tanks. The primary is a few turns of heavy
conductor plus the tank capacitor. The secondary is a thousand turns of fine
wire, and its capacitance is not a component at all --- it is the topload and
the coil's own self-capacitance against the surrounding air. \textbf{Tuning a
Tesla coil means adjusting one tank until its $f_0$ matches the other's}, and
you do it by moving the primary's tap, which changes $L$. Every hour you will
later spend tuning the coil is this lesson.
\end{telospanel}
