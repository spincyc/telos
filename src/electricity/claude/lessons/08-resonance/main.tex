\telosmasthead[Measure the peak; do not merely hunt for one]
\lessonhead{8}{two sessions}{learner: pendulum; adult: low-voltage electrical sweep}

\begin{materials}
\item Stand, string, small mass, ruler, tape flag
\item Stopwatch and partner to call crossings
\item Two identical cardstock damping fins
\item Adult-controlled, isolated signal generator
\item \(1\,\unitof{k\Omega}\) current-limiting resistor
\item Known film capacitor and inductor; AC meter or oscilloscope
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
Equal taps are delivered slower than, at, and faster than a pendulum's own
rhythm. Sketch amplitude versus tap rate. Where is its maximum? Now add a
cardstock fin: predict what changes about the maximum's height and width.
Finally predict how doubling \(C\) changes an LC circuit's preferred frequency.
\end{predict}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Measure the free rhythm.} Release from the same small tape mark,
without a push. Time 12 complete cycles from a same-direction centre crossing.
Repeat four times. For each trial calculate
\[
T_i=\frac{t_{12,i}}{12},\qquad
\bar T=\frac{T_1+T_2+T_3+T_4}{4},\qquad f_{\rm mech}=\frac{1}{\bar T}.
\]
The four \(T_i\) values must span no more than \(5\%\) of \(\bar T\). If they do,
repeat after checking the pivot, release mark, and cycle call.

\item \textbf{Calibrate one tap.} A partner calls the same-direction centre
crossing. Practise a short sideways finger tap with identical travel each time.
Do not chase the mass with the finger. Reset from the tape mark for every run.

\item \textbf{Sweep both sides.} Apply eight taps, separated by
\[
0.70\bar T,\quad0.85\bar T,\quad1.00\bar T,\quad1.15\bar T,\quad1.30\bar T.
\]
Measure the largest displacement after tap eight. Randomize the order of the
five settings; repeat the whole set three times. Random order helps expose
practice, fatigue, and a drifting tap size.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{Loss is evidence, not an inconvenience}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \textbf{Measure free decay.} With no taps, release from amplitude \(A_0\).
Count cycles until the amplitude first falls below \(A_0/2\); call that
\(N_{1/2}\). Repeat three times. Attach one fin and repeat, then two fins.
\item \textbf{Sweep with damping.} Repeat the five timing settings with one
fin. Use the same release, number of taps, and randomized order. Plot bare and
damped mean amplitudes on the same axes.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teloscard}{Questions before the electrical analogy}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which timing produced the greatest \emph{mean} response? Did response
fall on both sides, or did you merely stop searching at the largest point?
\item Did the best interval agree with \(\bar T\) within one tested step?
\item Were early and late settings equally far from \(1.00\bar T\) similar?
If not, what uncontrolled asymmetry could explain it?
\item Did fins reduce \(N_{1/2}\)? Did they lower and broaden the driven peak?
\item Which conclusion is supported: ``resonance makes energy,'' or
``phase controls how efficiently repeated taps transfer supplied energy''?
\end{enumerate}
\end{teloscard}

\lessonsection{Predict, calculate, sweep, repeat}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Record the component values and tolerances. Calculate
\[
f_{\rm calc}=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}.
\]
Calculate a starting sweep from \(0.50f_{\rm calc}\) to
\(1.50f_{\rm calc}\). Use at least nine points and place more points near the
predicted maximum.

\item With power off, the adult builds the pictured parallel LC network behind
the \(1\,\unitof{k\Omega}\) limiting resistor and checks the meter range. Keep
generator amplitude fixed and low. The learner records; the adult alone
operates and changes the energized circuit.

\item Sweep upward through all frequencies, then downward. At each point wait
the same settling time and record tank voltage. A real peak must survive sweep
direction. If the two readings differ materially, repeat after checking
generator amplitude, connection motion, meter bandwidth, and settling time.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Let \(f_{\rm meas}\) be the frequency of the largest mean voltage. Report
the fractional disagreement:
\[
\text{difference}(\%)=
100\frac{\left|f_{\rm meas}-f_{\rm calc}\right|}{f_{\rm calc}}.
\]
Do not call the formula wrong until component tolerance, coil resistance,
stray capacitance, meter loading, generator output, and frequency resolution
have been considered.

\item Add a known series resistor to the inductor branch and repeat the
up/down sweep. The expected damping signature is a lower, wider peak. If
\(f_1\) and \(f_2\) are the half-power frequencies from an oscilloscope-quality
measurement, estimate
\[
Q\approx\frac{f_{\rm meas}}{f_2-f_1}.
\]
With a simple AC multimeter, describe width qualitatively; do not claim a
precision \(Q\) its bandwidth and sampling cannot support.

\item Change \(C\) by a known factor and repeat. Before measuring, predict the
ratio
\[
\frac{f_{\rm new}}{f_{\rm old}}
=\sqrt{\frac{C_{\rm old}}{C_{\rm new}}}.
\]
For example, four times the capacitance predicts half the frequency.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{Final proof}

\begin{gate}{Accept the resonance claim only when all six agree}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Four free-period trials agree and define a mechanical prediction.
\item The mechanical response has an interior maximum and falls on both sides.
\item Added drag shortens free decay and lowers or broadens the driven response.
\item The electrical up/down sweeps reproduce an interior maximum.
\item \(f_{\rm meas}\) is compared numerically with \(1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})\), with
component and measurement limits stated.
\item Changing \(C\) moves the maximum in the square-root direction and by a
defensible amount.
\end{enumerate}
If any item fails, write \emph{not yet demonstrated}, identify the failed
control, and specify the next repeat. A tall reading by itself is not proof.
\end{gate}

\begin{telospanel}{What the analogy earns}
The pendulum and LC circuit share three measured signatures: a natural rate, a
two-sided driven-response maximum, and loss-dependent decay and peak width.
That supports a common oscillator model. It does not mean charge is a hanging
mass or that resonance supplies energy. The driver supplies energy; timing
governs accumulation; loss drains it.
\end{telospanel}

\begin{teloshazard}{Boundary}
The learner investigation ends with the pendulum. The electrical sweep uses
only an isolated, current-limited, low-voltage generator and is assembled
deenergized. The adult controls power and connections. No mains, opened
appliances, charged power capacitors, ignition coils, sparks, or exposed high
voltage belong in this lesson. Adult status does not imply electrical
qualification.
\end{teloshazard}
