\lessonhead{3}{about 90 minutes}{everyone builds; battery power only}

\begin{materials}
\item A digital multimeter (each, if possible)
\item Solderless breadboard and jumper wires
\item 9\,V battery and clip; two AA cells and a holder
\item Resistors: 100\,$\Omega$, 220\,$\Omega$, 470\,$\Omega$, 1\,k$\Omega$, 10\,k$\Omega$
\item Several LEDs
\item A small torch bulb and holder
\item A toggle or push switch
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
An LED is connected to a 9\,V battery through a 470\,$\Omega$ resistor. You then
swap it for a 220\,$\Omega$ resistor. Does the LED get brighter, dimmer, or stay
the same --- and roughly by how much? Write a number, not a word.
\end{predict}

\lessonsection{Do it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Build the loop.} Battery, switch, resistor, LED, back to the
  battery. The LED only works one way round --- if nothing lights, turn it
  around before you assume anything is broken.
\item \textbf{Measure voltage.} Voltage is measured \emph{across} a component,
  so the meter goes in parallel: red probe one side, black the other, without
  disturbing the circuit. Measure across the battery, across the resistor, and
  across the LED. Add the last two together and compare with the first.
\item \textbf{Measure current.} Current is measured \emph{through} the circuit,
  so the meter must become part of the loop: break the circuit and put the meter
  in the gap. Record it in milliamps.
\item \textbf{Swap resistors.} Repeat for 100, 220, 470, 1\,k and
  10\,k$\Omega$. Fill in the table: $R$, measured $V$ across the resistor, and
  measured $I$. Then compute $V/I$ for each row.
\item \textbf{Series and parallel.} Two 470\,$\Omega$ resistors in a row, then
  side by side. Measure the total resistance of each arrangement before you
  reason about it.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{What it means}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{The three quantities}{\textbf{Voltage} is the push, measured between
  two points. \textbf{Current} is the flow, measured through one point.
  \textbf{Resistance} is how hard the path fights the flow.}
\telosfact{Ohm's law}{$V = I R$. Your $V/I$ column should have come out close to
  the marked resistance every time. You did not assume the law --- you measured
  it into existence, which is the correct order.}
\telosfact{Voltages add around a loop}{Your resistor and LED voltages summed to
  the battery voltage. Every volt the battery supplies is spent somewhere before
  the current gets back.}
\telosfact{Current is the same all the way round}{A series loop has one current.
  Nothing is used up; charge that leaves comes back.}
\telosfact{Why the LED needs the resistor}{An LED is not a resistor --- above
  its turn-on voltage its current rises almost without limit. The resistor sets
  the current. Without it the LED passes as much as the battery can give and
  destroys itself. Try it once, deliberately, with a spare.}
\telosfact{Series and parallel}{In series, resistances add. In parallel the
  total is \emph{smaller} than either --- two paths carry more than one.}
\end{telosfacts}

\lessonsection{Habits to build now}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{One hand}{Start now, at 9\,V where it does not matter. Meter probe in
  one hand, other hand off the bench. By Lesson 11 you will not have to think
  about it.}
\telosfact{Meter mode first}{Look at the dial before every measurement. A meter
  left in current mode and then put across a battery is a short circuit through
  a fuse, and on a big supply it is an explosion.}
\telosfact{Power off to rewire}{Disconnect the battery before changing the
  circuit, every time, even though nothing here can hurt you.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{The link to the coil}
Everything you will build later is measured with this meter and reasoned about
with this law. The bleeder resistors that drain the capacitor bank, the current
limit of the transformer, the safety of a discharge --- all of it is $V = IR$
with big numbers. \qty{10}{mA} through a person is roughly what kills them;
work out for yourself what resistance across \qty{9}{kV} that implies, and
whether dry skin will save you.
\end{telospanel}
