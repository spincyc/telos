\telosmasthead[Build an electroscope, then make it prove the claim]
\lessonhead{1}{two sessions of about 75 minutes}
  {everyone builds; no electrical hazard}

\begin{materials}
\item Sticky tape (regular, not invisible)
\item Two balloons and string
\item Glass jar with plastic or cork lid
\item Stiff copper wire, about 20\,cm
\item Aluminium foil; ruler; protractor
\item Plastic comb or PVC offcut
\item Wool sweater or silk scarf
\item Empty aluminium can
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
Commit now. Two tape strips pulled from the same bench will
\(\bigcirc\) attract \(\bigcirc\) repel \(\bigcirc\) do nothing. A charged
balloon will pull a neutral can because \rule{.42\linewidth}{.3pt}
\end{predict}

\lessonsection{The rules of this test}

Static charge is feeble, leaky and easy to fake with a draught. That is useful:
it forces good experimental habits.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Shut the window. Move loose paper away. Record whether the day is dry,
  average or humid.
\item For every tape test, use strips \(15\) cm long with a \(2\) cm folded
  handle. Touch the handle only.
\item Record the \emph{first} movement. If the tapes touch, discharge both on
  the bench and start that trial again.
\item Repeat every claimed effect three times. One theatrical success proves
  nothing.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{A. Find the two signs}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Hang two unprepared strips from pencils balanced across cups. Set their
  lower ends \(2\) cm apart. Wait ten seconds. This is the zero trial.
\item Press two fresh strips separately onto the same clean bench. Smooth each
  ten times. Pull each off briskly and hang it at once.
\item Begin \(8\) cm apart. Bring the stands inward \(1\) cm every three
  seconds. Write the gap at the first repeatable motion and its direction.
\item Discharge, repeat twice, and swap the stands left-to-right for trial 3.
  Did the result follow the tape or the side of the room?
\end{enumerate}

\telosmasthead[Lesson 1 --- separate a pair, then challenge the result]
\lessonsection{B. Give two tapes opposite histories}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Put a fresh strip on the bench and mark its handle \(B\), for bottom.
  Press a second strip on top and mark it \(T\), for top.
\item Lift the joined pair by \(B\). Before peeling, answer: is the pair as a
  whole charged, neutral, or not yet known? \rule{.25\linewidth}{.3pt}
\item Peel \(T\) from \(B\) in one clean pull. Hang both at once. Record their
  first motion from \(8,7,6,\ldots\) cm.
\item Make a second pair. Compare \(T_1\) with \(T_2\), then \(B_1\) with
  \(B_2\), resetting between comparisons.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{telospanel}{Stop and argue}
Attraction cannot, by itself, prove opposite net charges: a charged object can
attract a neutral one. Which tape result \emph{cannot} be explained by a
neutral partner? Why is swapping the stands a useful control?
\end{telospanel}

\telosmasthead[Lesson 1 --- neutral things still move]
\lessonsection{C. Polarisation: water and aluminium}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Lay the empty can on a level bench and mark its starting line. With the
  balloon uncharged and \(2\) cm away, verify that the can stays put.
\item Rub the balloon on wool exactly ten strokes. Approach the can from
  \(12\) cm in \(1\) cm steps, pausing three seconds. Never touch it.
\item Record the first gap that starts the can rolling. Recharge with ten
  strokes and repeat twice. Then touch the balloon to the bench and repeat as a
  discharged control.
\item Run a water stream about \(2\) mm wide. Measure the sideways deflection
  \(5\) cm below the tap with the charged balloon held \(3\) cm away.
\item Discharge the balloon and repeat. Do not report ``it bent''; report
  direction and millimetres.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{telospanel}{The question that matters}
The can began neutral. If it still moved toward the balloon, attraction is not
proof of opposite net charge. The nearer charges rearranged or turned; the
farther ones could not cancel the stronger near-side interaction.
\end{telospanel}

\telosmasthead[Lesson 1 --- build the instrument]
\lessonsection{D. Build an electroscope}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Ask the adult to make a close-fitting hole through the lid. Push
  \(20\) cm of stiff copper wire through it. The wire must not touch a metal lid.
\item Bend the inner \(3\) cm into a narrow hook. Cut two identical foil leaves,
  each \(1\times4\) cm. Round their lower corners.
\item Hang both leaves on the hook so their flat faces touch. Crumple foil
  tightly around the outer end to make a smooth \(2\) cm ball.
\item Close the jar. The leaves must hang freely, clear of glass and lid.
  Tape a paper protractor behind the jar with \(0^\circ\) on the centreline.
\item Zero test: touch the ball for three seconds. Wait ten seconds. Record the
  resting separation angle. It should be \(0^\circ\) or close to it.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{E. Make three distinct states}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Contact charge.} Rub the comb ten strokes. Touch it to the ball
  for one second; remove it. Record leaf angle at 0, 10, 30 and 60 seconds.
\item Touch the ball with one finger for three seconds. The leaves should
  collapse. This is the reset, not an optional flourish.
\item \textbf{Near, no touch.} Recharge the comb. Hold it \(2\) cm from the ball.
  Record the leaf angle. Withdraw it. Record the angle again.
\item \textbf{Charge by induction.} Bring the comb back to \(2\) cm. While it
  stays there, touch the ball. Remove your finger \emph{first}; remove the comb
  second. Record the final angle.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{The proof, not the performance}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two signs.} Like-history pairs repelled in
  \rule{.8cm}{.3pt}/3 trials. The opposite-history matrix showed
  \rule{2.2cm}{.3pt}. Repulsion rules out a neutral second object.
\item \textbf{Conservation.} \(T\) and \(B\) began as one system and acquired
  opposite behaviours together. State what moved and where the balancing
  charge remained: \rule{.48\linewidth}{.3pt}
\item \textbf{Polarisation.} The neutral can began moving at a median gap of
  \rule{.8cm}{.3pt} cm; the discharged control \rule{1.8cm}{.3pt}. Therefore
  attraction alone does not identify net sign.
\item \textbf{Conduction.} Contact left a leaf angle after the comb departed;
  the near-only state did not. The difference is charge transfer.
\item \textbf{Induction.} Removing the earth path first trapped a final leaf
  angle of \rule{.8cm}{.3pt}\(^\circ\). Reversing the removal order should erase
  it. Do that control once.
\end{enumerate}

\telosmasthead[Lesson 1 --- conclusions and the link forward]
\begin{telospanel}{Five questions before you claim victory}
Which observation proves like signs? Which control separates charge from a
draught? Why does the neutral can move? Why must finger-before-comb matter?
Which measured result was least repeatable, and what limits it?
\end{telospanel}

\lessonsection{What it means}

Charge has two signs. Like signs repel; unlike signs attract. Rubbing and
peeling transfer electrons between objects, leaving equal and opposite changes
inside the larger system. Conductors let charge travel; insulators slow that
travel. A nearby charge can rearrange charge without touching. The electroscope
makes each of those sentences visible, but only the controls make them evidence.

\begin{telospanel}{The link to the coil}
The topload of a Tesla coil is a smooth conductor that stores charge. A
streamer is charge escaping into air. Your electroscope shows the same family
of effects at a vastly smaller energy. Similar physics is not similar risk.
\end{telospanel}
