\telosmasthead[The rules that do not bend]

\begin{teloshazard}{What you are dealing with}
A neon-sign transformer turns household power into \qtyrange{9}{15}{kV} at
\qtyrange{30}{60}{mA}. Roughly \qty{10}{mA} across the chest can stop a heart,
and \qty{100}{mA} very reliably does. This supply delivers several times that,
it is a \emph{current-limited but not current-safe} source, and it will not let
go of you --- above about \qty{15}{mA} your hand muscles clamp shut on the
conductor. The tank capacitor stores a lethal charge \textbf{after the power is
off} and will hold it for a long time. Nothing in Lessons 1--9 can hurt you.
Everything from Lesson 10 can kill an adult.
\end{teloshazard}

\section{The five rules}

\begin{telosfacts}[0.06]
\telosfact{1}{\textbf{The adult owns the plug.} One adult, named in advance, is
  the only person who plugs in, switches on, or decides that the machine may be
  energized. That does not rotate, and it does not get delegated because someone
  is busy. Everyone else treats the machine as live until that person says
  otherwise.}
\telosfact{2}{\textbf{Unplug, then short, then touch.} Never in another order,
  and never skipping a step. Pull the plug from the wall --- not the switch,
  the plug. Then take the grounding stick and short the capacitor terminals
  together, and \emph{leave it shorted} while your hands are inside. A capacitor
  that was discharged five minutes ago can recover a dangerous voltage on its
  own through dielectric absorption. Short it again every time.}
\telosfact{3}{\textbf{One hand.} When anything is or might be energized, your
  other hand goes in your pocket or behind your back. Current that enters one
  hand and leaves the other crosses your heart. Current that enters and leaves
  the same hand mostly does not. This is a habit built in Lesson 3, when it
  costs nothing, precisely so it is automatic in Lesson 11.}
\telosfact{4}{\textbf{Never alone.} The machine is never energized, or capable
  of being energized, with one person in the room. The second person's job is
  to be able to pull the plug and call for help. They should know where the plug
  is without looking.}
\telosfact{5}{\textbf{Stand back and stay back.} Once it is running, nobody is
  within arm's reach of any part of it. Adjustments happen with the plug out and
  the capacitor shorted. There is no adjustment worth making while it runs.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Build these in, not on}

\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Grounding stick}{A length of dry hardwood or PVC with a metal hook at
  the end, a heavy wire from the hook to your RF ground. Build it \emph{first},
  before any high-voltage part is assembled. It is the single most important
  object in the room.}
\telosfact{Bleeder resistors}{Every capacitor in the bank gets a high-value
  resistor permanently across it, so the bank drains on its own in seconds
  rather than holding a charge for hours. Bleeders are a backup for the
  grounding stick, never a replacement for it.}
\telosfact{Safety gap}{A fixed spark gap wired across the transformer output,
  set slightly wider than the main gap. The coil's tank circuit can reflect
  voltage spikes back into the transformer and destroy it; the safety gap fires
  first and shunts them. It protects the equipment, and equipment that fails
  unexpectedly is a hazard to people.}
\telosfact{Interlocked enclosure}{The transformer, capacitor and gap live inside
  a box. The box has a switch that cuts mains power when it is opened. Test the
  interlock deliberately before you trust it.}
\telosfact{A real switch}{A proper switched and fused enclosure with an
  indicator lamp, at a distance from the coil. Not a power strip on the floor.}
\telosfact{RF ground}{The secondary's base wire goes to \emph{its own ground
  rod} driven outside, not to the mains earth in a wall outlet. Streamers
  returning through house wiring will destroy electronics elsewhere in the
  building and can energize things that should not be energized.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{The other hazards, which are easy to forget}

\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Pacemakers and implants}{A running Tesla coil produces intense
  electromagnetic fields. Anyone with a pacemaker, implanted defibrillator,
  insulin pump, cochlear implant or hearing aid must not be in or near the
  room. This is not a precaution to weigh against convenience --- ask everyone
  before you fire it, every time.}
\telosfact{Electronics}{Phones, watches, laptops, hearing aids and car keys go
  in another room. Not in a pocket. The coil does not distinguish between an
  antenna and a phone.}
\telosfact{Radio interference}{A spark-gap coil is a broadband radio
  transmitter, and an inefficient one. It will interfere with radio, television
  and wireless service well beyond your own house, and unintentional radiators
  are regulated. Run it in short bursts, keep total runtime low, and stop if a
  neighbour reports interference.}
\telosfact{Ozone and nitrogen oxides}{Sparks in air make both, and both are
  lung irritants. Ventilate the space, and do not run it in a closed basement
  room. If you can smell it strongly, stop and air the room out.}
\telosfact{Ultraviolet}{An arc emits UV. Do not stare into the gap. Safety
  glasses, and the gap enclosed or shielded.}
\telosfact{Noise}{A spark gap is genuinely loud and the sound is impulsive.
  Hearing protection for everyone in the room.}
\telosfact{Fire}{You are making sparks near wood, dust and insulation. A fire
  extinguisher within reach, a clear bench, and nothing flammable near the gap.}
\telosfact{Burns}{Streamers are hot enough to burn skin instantly and to ignite
  paper. The RF burn from a streamer is deep and does not hurt at first, because
  it destroys nerve endings on the way in.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Before every run --- read it aloud}

\begin{telosframe}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item Is the grounding stick in the room and within reach?
\item Is the capacitor bank shorted right now, and are my hands out?
\item Is the enclosure closed and the interlock tested?
\item Is the RF ground connected to the ground rod, and the connection tight?
\item Has everyone with an implant left, and are all electronics out of the room?
\item Is there a second person, and do they know where the plug is?
\item Safety glasses and hearing protection on everyone?
\item Is the extinguisher within reach and the bench clear?
\item Is everyone at least an arm's length beyond the longest streamer I expect?
\item Am I the named adult, and am I the one about to close this switch?
\end{enumerate}
\end{telosframe}

\begin{teloshazard}{If someone is shocked}
Do not touch them. \textbf{Kill the power first} --- pull the plug --- because
if they are still in contact with the conductor you will be the second casualty.
Then call emergency services. Any electrical shock across the chest warrants
medical assessment even if the person feels fine afterwards: electrical injury
causes heart rhythm disturbances that can appear hours later, and internal
burns that are invisible from outside. ``He said he was fine'' is not a
clinical assessment.
\end{teloshazard}

\begin{telospanel}{Why the rules are written this way}
Every rule above exists because it is a habit, not a judgement. In the moment
that something goes wrong you will not reason your way to the right action ---
you will do whatever you have always done. That is the entire reason Lessons
1--9 use batteries that cannot hurt anyone and still insist on one hand,
discharge before touching, and a second person in the room. The rules are the
point of the low-voltage lessons; the physics is a bonus.
\end{telospanel}
