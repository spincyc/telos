\telosmasthead[Claude edition --- the boundary comes first]

\begin{teloshazard}{Learner limit}
Learner investigations use only isolated battery sources at \qty{12}{V DC} or
less. Outlets, mains leads, opened appliances, large battery packs, charged
power capacitors, ignition coils, sparks, and exposed high voltage are not
learner work. An adult facilitator controls the bounded battery activity; adult
status does not make anyone electrically qualified.
\end{teloshazard}

\begin{teloscard}{Three roles---never collapse them}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{.18\linewidth}L@{}}
\textbf{Learner} & Predicts, assembles only the approved battery circuit while
deenergized, observes, records, and obeys every stop call.\\
\textbf{Adult facilitator} & Selects materials, verifies the boundary and meter
setup, owns the switch and timing decision, and ends the trial at uncertainty.\\
\textbf{Qualified person} & Performs work whose voltage, equipment, environment,
or rules require electrical qualification. Being the learner's parent, teacher,
or supervising adult does not confer this role.
\end{tabularx}
\end{teloscard}

\begin{telospanel}{Boundary questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The label says \(9\) V, but the pack is large and its current capability
is unknown. Which part of the boundary rejects it?
\item An appliance is unplugged. Does that remove stored energy, damaged
insulation, or the need for qualification?
\item An adult offers to make the connection. Does changing the hands performing
the work change the work's category?
\end{enumerate}
\end{telospanel}

\section{Preflight: point, name, verify}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{.21\linewidth}p{.36\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Gate} & \textbf{Learner points and says} & \textbf{Adult verifies}\\
\midrule
Source & Type, cell count, stated voltage & Isolated holder; correct intact
cells; no swelling, leaks, dents, corrosion, heat, or mixed chemistries\\
Workspace & Switch, disconnect, parts tray & Dry, bright, clear of drinks,
jewelry, loose metal, and bystanders; eye protection in place\\
Conductors & Every intended bare end & Insulation sound; strands contained;
no possible bridge across source terminals\\
Load & Part name and expected behavior & Correct value/rating; LED has its
series resistor; coil or resistor shows no prior heat damage\\
Control & Who owns the switch; trial duration & Switch begins open; disconnect
reachable; timer and cool-down interval ready\\
Evidence & Predicted sign, direction, or range & Prediction is specific enough
to distinguish expected, null, and surprising results\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{gate}{Decision}
If a label, rating, connection, meter setting, or expected result is uncertain,
the source remains disconnected. ``Probably'' is a failed preflight.
\end{gate}

\textbf{Checkpoint.} One copper strand nearly reaches the other terminal. It has
not touched. Explain why this is already a failed conductor gate.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Inspect the case, lead insulation, and probe guards. Remove a damaged
meter or lead from service; tape is not a bench repair.
\item Put black in \textsc{com}. Put red in the jack named by the lesson. Select
the quantity and a range that can contain the predicted value.
\item Rehearse voltage by touching the two named nodes with the empty holder.
Rehearse current by opening the named path and inserting the meter there.
\item Never place a current-configured meter across a source. Return the red
lead from a current jack when the current measurement ends.
\item Adult introduces one cold fault: wrong jack, wrong mode, open connection,
or reversed LED. Learner finds it by inspection, never by trial power.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teloscard}{Intermediate meter questions}
The display remains zero. Could that mean a true zero, open switch, loose
connection, wrong range, or wrong placement? What must happen before moving a
lead? \textbf{Open the switch and disconnect.}\par
The expected current is \(18\) mA. The probes are laid across the holder. Why
must the adult stop the trial even though the source is within the voltage limit?
\end{teloscard}

\section{One controlled energizing cycle}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Adult completes the preflight and calls ``ready.'' Learner's hands clear
the circuit. The switch owner calls ``on'' and starts the stated interval.
\item Observe only the planned quantity. Never reposition, squeeze, substitute,
or add a part while energized.
\item Switch open; source disconnected; adult checks for unexpected heating.
Only then may a lead, probe, load, core, or magnet move.
\item Record the result with units and trial time. A null or surprising result
begins a deenergized diagnosis; it never authorizes more voltage.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teloshazard}{Stop means the trial is over}
Stop for unexpected warmth, a hot cell or lead, swelling, leakage, sharp odor,
smoke, discoloration, buzzing, sparking, pain or tingling, damaged insulation,
a dropped metal object, unstable readings, or behavior outside the prediction.
Open the switch if safe, disconnect without touching the suspect part, isolate
the setup, and tell the adult. Do not repeat ``just once.''
\end{teloshazard}

\section{Fault decisions}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{.25\linewidth}p{.30\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Observation} & \textbf{Immediate action} & \textbf{Cold diagnosis}\\
\midrule
No response & Open, disconnect, preserve layout & Compare each node with the
drawing; check continuity and polarity; do not raise source voltage\\
Meter overload or jumping digits & Disconnect before touching probes & Recheck
jack, mode, range, placement, and predicted magnitude\\
Part warms more each trial & Stop and leave it untouched & Check value, wiring,
current, duration, and cool-down; replace damaged parts\\
Cell heats, leaks, smells, dents, or swells & Adult clears and isolates the area
& Follow cell-maker and local disposal guidance; never reuse or recharge it\\
Metal bridges source terminals & Disconnect if safe; never grab hot metal &
Replace damaged holder, switch, cells, or leads; rebuild from the start\\
Small magnet or clip is missing & Stop and count all pieces & Find it before
anyone leaves; store magnets inaccessible to children\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{telospanel}{Falsification questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item What evidence would show that an apparent ``bad resistor'' is actually an
open switch or lead?
\item Three timed coil trials lift fewer clips each time. Name a thermal change
that could matter even though turns and source stayed the same.
\item Why does an unexpected result count against the setup, not against the
safety boundary?
\end{enumerate}
\end{telospanel}

\section{Cold verification drill}

Use an approved lesson circuit with the holder empty. Learner speaks and points;
adult evaluates without prompting.

\begin{enumerate}
\item State source type and voltage; identify switch, load, and disconnect.
\item Trace the intended current path and the most plausible accidental short.
\item Demonstrate both meter placements and restore the red lead to
V/\(\Omega\) afterward.
\item Name five stop observations and show where hands go when ``stop'' is called.
\item Find an adult-created harmless fault by working through the gates in
order. Do not insert cells to diagnose it.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{gate}{Verification---every box before cells enter}
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Isolated source \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} \(\leq 12\) V DC \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Correct parts \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Intact cells/leads \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Switch open \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Shorts removed \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Meter verified \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Prediction written \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Time stated \quad
\fbox{\rule{0pt}{.7ex}\rule{.7ex}{0pt}} Stop signs named
\end{gate}
