\begin{teloshazard}{Boundary: a coupled-coil instrument, not a Tesla coil}
Use only 2--4 AA cells in a fused, switched holder: \qty{6}{V} DC maximum.
No outlet, mains lead, plug-in transformer, charged capacitor, ignition coil,
spark gap, flyback transformer, large battery pack, exposed high voltage, or
attempt to make a streamer. The primary receives brief battery pulses. The
electrically isolated secondary connects only to a voltmeter or a correctly
resistor-limited antiparallel LED pair. Disconnect the holder for every change.
\end{teloshazard}

\section{Gate 0 --- freeze the design}
Choose two dry cardboard bobbins that accept the same removable core. Plan
100--200 turns per coil and begin with equal counts, so geometry rather than
turn ratio is the first variable. Draw and label P1/P2 and S1/S2. Decide where
the fuse, OFF label, strain relief, and measurement points will be.

\coilstateplate

\begin{predict}
Where could the enamel scrape? What prevents a loose loop, a pulled lead, or
two bare terminals touching? Point to a control for each failure before
touching materials.
\end{predict}

\section{Gate 1 --- wind, count, and prove each coil}
\begin{enumerate}
\item With no battery holder on the bench, round or tape every bobbin edge.
Measure the winding width and core clearance.
\item Leave a \qty{10}{cm} lead and tape it as P1. Wind in one direction with
neat adjacent turns. Count aloud in tens and put a tally mark on paper for every
ten. Do not use a powered drill.
\item Tape the finished layer without hiding either end. Leave \qty{10}{cm}
for P2. Repeat for S1/S2. Record both turn counts.
\item The adult removes enamel from only the last \qty{1}{cm} of each lead.
With the meter on ohms, record \(R_P\) and \(R_S\). Gently flex one lead at a
time. Each reading must remain finite and stable.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{predict}
If the meter says OL, is the winding continuous? If it reads nearly zero, what
accidental short path must be ruled out? Why can a turn count not substitute
for this electrical proof?
\end{predict}

\section{Gate 2 --- assemble with power absent}
Fasten the holder, master switch, pushbutton, bobbins, and leads to a cardboard
base. Add strain relief before joining conductors. Wire
holder--fuse--master--button--primary--holder. Connect the secondary only to
the voltmeter. Slide the core through both bobbins and confirm that it cannot
touch a terminal.

The adult traces both circuits aloud. With cells removed, continuity mode must
show: master OFF is open; master ON plus button pressed completes only the
primary; P1/P2 has no continuity to S1/S2 in any of the four pairings.

\begin{predict}
Why must the two circuits remain electrically isolated even though energy
passes between them? If one P-to-S pairing beeps, list three possible causes.
\end{predict}

\section{Gate 3 --- verify one controlled pulse}
\pulseplate

Set the secondary meter to a low DC-voltage range. Start with the bobbins
coaxial and touching, with the core fully inserted. The adult installs two AA
cells, reads the holder voltage, and turns the master ON. The learner presses
for less than one second, releases, and records the largest visible indication
at both edges. Switch OFF and disconnect the holder. Repeat three times only
after confirming holder and coil remain at room temperature.

\begin{predict}
Why can press and release show opposite signs? Why is holding the button down
not a stronger induction test? Name every observation that means stop now.
\end{predict}

\section{Gate 4 --- change one thing}
Choose one variable: core in/out, measured gap, coil angle, or cell count
within 2--4 AA cells. Hold turn counts, pulse length, meter range, and lead
orientation fixed. Make three trials at each setting. Before every reposition:
master OFF, holder disconnected, then check for warmth and insulation damage.

\section{Diagnostic tree --- no repeatable receiver indication}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No source proof:} disconnect coils. Adult checks holder voltage,
fuse, master, and button. Do not reconnect until the power path passes.
\item \textbf{Primary open:} cells out; remeasure P1--P2 while flexing leads.
OL or a jumping value means clean the ends, repair, or rewind.
\item \textbf{Receiver open or meter wrong:} remeasure S1--S2; confirm COM/V
jacks and volts mode, never the amps jack.
\item \textbf{Isolation failed:} check all P-to-S pairs. Any continuity means
stop and find the contact or stray strand.
\item \textbf{Coupling weak:} return to zero gap, aligned bobbins, core fully
inserted, and one brief pulse.
\item \textbf{Display missed the transient:} reverse S1/S2 and watch both press
and release. A slow meter is a measurement limit, never permission to raise
voltage or make sparks.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teloshazard}{Stop line}
Stop for warmth, odor, damaged enamel, a loose lead, a repeatedly opened fuse,
or any unexplained result. Do not bypass the fuse. Do not substitute a wall
supply, capacitor, large battery, ignition coil, flyback, or spark gap.
\end{teloshazard}

\section{Explain from the evidence}
A primary current creates a magnetic field. Only a \emph{change} in that
current changes the linked flux and induces a secondary voltage, so the useful
events are press and release. Alignment, gap, a shared magnetic core, and turn
count change how much flux links the windings. This is mutual induction made
visible without a shock, spark, or uncontrolled energy store.
