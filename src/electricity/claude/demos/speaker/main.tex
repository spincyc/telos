\begin{telospanel}{Boundary before the build}
Use only a battery-powered, current-limited audio amplifier, initially at
minimum level. The coil connects through a 100 \(\Omega\), 1 W resistor. An
adult secures the magnet and controls power. Disconnect for every resistance,
alignment, or wiring check. Stop for heat, odor, pain, damaged insulation,
distortion, or a magnet that can move.
\end{telospanel}

\begin{materials}
\item 30--32 AWG enamelled wire; card former; sandpaper
\item Paper cup, low-tack tape, narrow paper motion flag
\item Ceramic magnet, or neodymium magnet secured by the adult
\item Battery audio amplifier and tone source; 100 \(\Omega\), 1 W resistor
\item Digital multimeter; ruler; optional spectrum-display app
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
Draw the cup position for positive, zero, and negative coil current. Will a
steady current make a continuing note? Which check should be made before power:
sound, resistance, or frequency? Predict where an added paper clip will move
the cup's strongest response.
\end{predict}

\lessonsection{Qualify the build in stages}
\subsection{A. Electrical check --- power disconnected}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Scrape both tails to bright copper. Measure the meter leads touching each
other, then measure across the coil. Record \(R_{\rm leads}\) and
\(R_{\rm measured}\); estimate \(R_{\rm coil}=R_{\rm measured}-R_{\rm leads}\).
\item Flex each tail gently while watching the display. A stable finite reading
is the pass condition. Open circuit means enamel or a break; a near-zero result
means verify the range and look for a bypass.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{B. Mechanical check --- still unpowered}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Tape the coil flat and concentric to the cup. Adult secures the magnet.
Move the cup lightly through its expected travel. Listen and feel: silence,
free motion, and equal clearance pass; scrape or sticking fails.
\item Attach a tiny paper flag to the rim. Confirm the 100 \(\Omega\) resistor,
insulation, and minimum volume before the adult permits power.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{C. Motion and response}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Play 25 Hz briefly. Raise level only until the flag's alternating motion
is visible. Ask: why can motion be visible before a clear pitch is heard?
\item Without changing the level, try 100, 200, 400, 800, and 1200 Hz. Mark
sound as 0 (none), 1 (faint), 2 (clear), or 3 (strong). If the meter is rated
for the frequency, record AC volts across the coil; otherwise write
``bandwidth not established.''
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{Final proof}
The speaker is demonstrated only when every claim has its own evidence:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\(\circ\),leftmargin=1.8em]
\item \textbf{Electrical:} coil resistance is finite, repeatable, and stable
under a gentle tail-flex test.
\item \textbf{Mechanical:} the unpowered cup travels freely and silently.
\item \textbf{Transduction:} 25 Hz produces visible alternating motion.
\item \textbf{Acoustic:} at least two commanded frequencies produce repeatable,
distinguishable sound without a stop condition.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{telospanel}{Explain only what the evidence earns}
Alternating current in the permanent magnet's field gives the coil an
alternating force. The cup follows and makes alternating pressure in the air.
Steady DC gives at most a one-time displacement plus heating, not a continuous
tone. Response is not flat: electrical impedance, moving mass, stiffness,
magnetic clearance, cup resonances, and hearing all affect observed level.
Resistance proves continuity; motion proves force reached the cup; repeatable
sound proves air was driven. No one observation proves the whole chain.
\end{telospanel}
