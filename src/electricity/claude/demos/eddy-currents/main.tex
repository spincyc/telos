\begin{predict}
Copper is not magnetic --- a magnet will not stick to it, and you can check that
first. The magnet never touches the pipe wall. So what could possibly be slowing
it down? Write an answer before you drop anything, and time both falls.
\end{predict}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.22]
\telosfact{What happens}{A falling magnet changes the magnetic field through each ring of the pipe wall as it passes. By Faraday's law that induces a voltage, and because the pipe is a good conductor, real current flows in circular loops --- eddies --- around the magnet.}
\telosfact{Which way}{By Lenz's law, always the direction that \emph{opposes} the change. The loop ahead of the magnet repels it; the loop behind attracts it. Both slow the fall.}
\telosfact{Terminal velocity}{The braking force grows with speed, so the magnet settles at a constant slow speed rather than accelerating. Exactly like a parachute, with no air involved.}
\telosfact{Where the energy goes}{Into heat in the pipe. The magnet loses gravitational energy and the copper warms up by an unmeasurably small amount. Nothing is lost.}
\end{telosfacts}

\lessonsection{Four versions, in this order}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The pipe.} Drop a magnet down copper, and a non-magnetic slug of the same weight beside it. Time both. Then aluminium, then cardboard.
\item \textbf{The pendulum.} Swing a magnet on a string just above a thick aluminium plate. It stops within a couple of swings. Lift it 2\,cm and it swings freely --- the effect falls off sharply with distance.
\item \textbf{The slide.} Tilt the aluminium plate and slide a strong magnet down it. It creeps. Put a sheet of paper between and it still creeps: nothing is touching.
\item \textbf{The spinning disc.} Balance an aluminium pie dish on a nail point and spin a magnet underneath it, close but not touching. The dish follows the magnet round. This is an induction motor.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{Where this is used}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.22]
\telosfact{Roller-coaster brakes}{Fins of copper passing between magnets. No contact, no pads, nothing to wear out, and it cannot fail closed.}
\telosfact{Train brakes}{The same idea on a much larger scale on high-speed rail.}
\telosfact{Your electricity meter}{The old spinning-disc type is exactly demonstration 4 running backwards.}
\telosfact{Metal detectors}{They induce eddy currents in buried metal and listen for the field those currents make.}
\telosfact{Induction hobs}{Eddy currents induced directly in the pan, heating it while the hob stays cool.}
\telosfact{Why transformers are laminated}{Because here eddy currents are pure loss. Splitting the core into insulated sheets breaks the loops. \sheetref{Transformers}}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{The sentence to make them say out loud}
``The magnet is being slowed down by a magnetic field that its own falling
created.'' Nothing touches it, copper is not magnetic, and there is no battery
anywhere. Once a child can say that sentence and mean it, they understand
induction better than most adults.
\end{telospanel}
