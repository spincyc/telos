\item Bare wire for the cross-connections

\begin{predict}
Water is not charged. There is no battery anywhere in this apparatus. So where
could the energy for a spark possibly come from? Commit to an answer, then look
at the height the water falls from.
\end{predict}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.22]
\telosfact{The seed}{One side starts with a tiny random charge imbalance --- there is always some.}
\telosfact{Induction does the rest}{Say can A is slightly negative. It is wired to ring \emph{B}, so ring B is slightly negative, which repels electrons from the drops forming inside it. Those drops fall slightly \textbf{positive} into can B. Can B is wired to ring A, making it positive, so drops through ring A fall \textbf{negative} into can A --- making A more negative than it started.}
\telosfact{Runaway}{Each side makes the other side stronger. The charge grows exponentially until the air between the cans breaks down. This is positive feedback made visible.}
\telosfact{Where the energy comes from}{Gravity. Each drop is pulled away from a can that is attracting it, so falling water does work against the electric field. It is a generator whose fuel is height.}
\telosfact{The voltage}{Thousands of volts, easily --- a visible, audible spark. The current is microscopic, so the shock is a sharp sting at worst.}
\end{telosfacts}

\lessonsection{Making it actually work}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.22]
\telosfact{Insulate everything}{This is the whole game. The cans must sit on genuinely dry insulators, and no wire may touch the frame. Charge leaks away faster than it is generated otherwise, and nothing happens.}
\telosfact{Dry day}{Humid air conducts. On a muggy August afternoon this will not work at all --- which is itself the lesson from Lesson 1.}
\telosfact{Cross the wires}{Can A to ring B, can B to ring A. Wire them straight across instead and it will never start. Get somebody to check this before you troubleshoot anything else.}
\telosfact{Drops, not a stream}{The stream must break into separate drops \emph{inside} the ring. A continuous jet conducts the charge straight back and defeats it.}
\telosfact{Be patient}{It can take thirty seconds to a couple of minutes to build to the first spark. Then it fires repeatedly.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{Why this is the best demonstration in the course}
It uses no electricity to make electricity. A jug of water on a shelf produces
visible sparks, and the only inputs are gravity and one accidental electron.
Lord Kelvin built this in 1867. It teaches induction, insulation, positive
feedback and energy conservation in one object --- and it is genuinely startling
the first time it cracks.
\end{telospanel}
