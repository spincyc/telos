\telosmasthead[Adult-controlled five-second AA-cell trials]
\lessonhead{Demo A}{20 minutes}{after Lesson 4 --- adult controls every contact}
\footnotesize

\begin{gate}{Stop line}
Use one AA alkaline cell only: never lithium, rechargeable cells, packs, or
larger batteries. This apparatus is nearly a direct short. The adult makes and
breaks contact, limits every trial to five seconds, and stops sooner for a
stall, spark, odor, pain, wobble, damage, or unexpected motion. Cool between
trials. Keep the magnet counted and away from small children, electronics, and
anyone with an implanted medical device.
\end{gate}

\begin{materials}
\item One fresh AA alkaline cell and one room-temperature reference cell
\item One unchipped neodymium disc magnet, 10--12\,mm
\item About 20\,cm of bare copper wire, 18--20 AWG
\item Pliers, timer, and paper direction flag
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
Sketch the view from above. If the magnet flips, should rotation reverse? If
both cell and magnet flip, should the result reverse or match the first trial?
Where in the three-part circuit will friction and heating appear?
\end{predict}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Adult counts and inspects the single magnet, then mounts it on the flat
negative end. Bend wire only while it is away from the cell.
\item Adult verifies symmetry, free clearance, a centered pivot, and two
feather-light rim contacts. The learner points to the complete current path and
identifies where excess pressure would add friction.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{2. Measure one baseline}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Put a paper direction flag behind the apparatus. Adult starts the timer
and lowers the wire; learner calls ``contact'' at \(0\) s, direction as seen
from above, start delay, and completed turns.
\item Adult lifts the wire at \(5\) s or at any stop condition. With the circuit
open, adult compares wire, cell, and magnet with the reference cell using the
back of a finger. Record \emph{same}, \emph{warmer}, or \emph{stop}; never test
temperature while current flows.
\item Cool at least \(60\) s. Stop for the day if any part is distinctly warm,
uncomfortable, swollen, leaking, discolored, chipped, or damaged.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{3. Predict and test reversals}
Keep geometry, time limit, stop line, and cool-down constant. Predict every row;
adult tests only the minimum stable combinations needed to check the pattern.

\begin{gate}{Stable assemblies only}
Reversing the cell requires remounting the magnet and reshaping the wire. If
the magnet will not seat securely on the opposite terminal, skip C and D.
Never tape a live contact, hold a spinning wire, or lengthen a trial.
\end{gate}

\lessonsection{Explain from the evidence}
Current in the wire crosses the magnet's field and experiences a sideways
force. The opposed arms turn that force into torque around the cell axis.
Reversing either current or field reverses rotation; reversing both preserves
it. Contact quality determines whether torque beats friction, not direction.
Most input energy can become heat, so timing and open-circuit temperature
comparisons are evidence.
