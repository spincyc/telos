\section{Four roles, one authority}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.18\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Adult lead} & Owns the launcher, fuel and igniter; calls every state
change; may stop the session without debate.\\
\textbf{Range watcher} & Watches the entire downrange area and backstop; says
``clear'' only after a fresh scan; calls ``CEASE FIRE'' on any change.\\
\textbf{Recorder} & Logs condition, shot number and observation from behind the
line. No approach until released by the adult lead.\\
\textbf{Everyone} & Eyes and double hearing protection on; stays behind the
line; repeats a cease-fire call so it cannot be missed.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{teloshazard}{Misfire branch}
Do not retry, add fuel, open, move, peer into, or approach. Adult lead calls
``MISFIRE---HOLD.'' Keep the muzzle downrange and every person behind the line
for a full \textbf{60 seconds}. Then the adult vents from the breech end while
remaining out of the muzzle line. A second anomaly ends the session.
\end{teloshazard}

\section{Stop means stop}
Stop immediately for a person, vehicle, animal, aircraft concern, shifting
wind/fire condition, lost projectile, unexpected sound, weak/odd discharge,
damaged or dropped launcher, loose fitting, fuel spill, PPE failure, or any
uncertainty. Unload and vent before discussion. Cracked, crazed, whitened,
deformed, badly UV-weathered, or heat-damaged PVC is retired, not repaired.

\begin{telospanel}{Why the range is larger than the equation}
The classroom trajectory assumes no air drag and a known launch state. A real
potato is irregular, draggy, variable, and capable of fragmenting. A calculation
is for learning; a walked empty range and positive earth backstop are for
safety. Never use a predicted landing point as a boundary.
\end{telospanel}
