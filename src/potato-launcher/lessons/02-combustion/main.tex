\begin{document}
\telosmasthead[The single most useful lesson in this project]
\lessonhead{2}{about 75 minutes}{adult handles all flames and fuel}

\begin{materials}
\item Candles, matches, glass jars of several sizes
\item A shallow dish of water
\item A tealight, a coin, and a bottle
\item Steel wool and a 9\,V battery
\item Flour or lycopodium powder, a candle, and a tube (outdoors)
\item Stopwatch
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
A candle burns under a jar. You use a jar twice the volume. Does it burn twice
as long, more than twice, or less? Write a number. Then: a chamber sprayed with
twice the fuel --- does it fire twice as hard?
\end{predict}

\lessonsection{Do it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Candle under a jar.} Time how long a candle burns under jars of different sizes. Plot time against volume. It is close to a straight line through the origin --- the candle is consuming a fixed \emph{ingredient}, and there is a fixed amount of it per litre of air.
\item \textbf{The rising water.} Stand a candle in a dish of water, light it, invert a jar over it. When it goes out, water rises into the jar. Roughly a fifth of the volume, which is the oxygen fraction of air.
\item \textbf{Fuel with no air.} Touch a 9\,V battery across steel wool. It burns fiercely --- iron is a fuel too, given enough surface area.
\item \textbf{Too much fuel.} Outdoors, with an adult: puff a small amount of flour through a candle flame and it flashes. Now try to light a \emph{pile} of flour with a match. It will not burn. Same substance, entirely different outcome.
\item \textbf{Suffocate one.} Light two candles under identical jars, but put a second lit candle under one of them. Predict, then time.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{What it means}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Burning needs three things}{Fuel, oxygen, and enough heat to start. Remove any one and it stops --- which is why the jar candle goes out, why the flour pile will not light, and why you fan the chamber between shots.}
\telosfact{Air is one-fifth oxygen}{About 21\%. The other 79\% is mostly nitrogen, which does not burn but does get heated --- and heated gas is exactly what pushes your potato, so the nitrogen is doing real work.}
\telosfact{The stoichiometric ratio}{There is one ratio of fuel to air at which both are used up exactly. Above it the mixture is \emph{rich}; below it, \emph{lean}. Both burn worse than the ideal, and far enough either way it will not ignite at all.}
\telosfact{Why more fuel is worse}{This is the whole lesson. A chamber with too much hairspray in it has no oxygen left to burn it with. It will not fire, and the next thing most people do --- add more --- makes it further from working, not closer. \sheetref{Safety}}
\telosfact{The flammability window is narrow}{For most hydrocarbons only a few per cent of fuel by volume in air will ignite at all. Your measured one-second burst is an attempt to land inside that window.}
\telosfact{Exhaust does not burn}{After a shot the chamber is full of carbon dioxide and water vapour. There is no oxygen left. Fanning it out is not superstition --- it is the difference between a shot and a misfire.}
\end{telosfacts}

\lessonsection{Break it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Two candles, one jar}{Half the time, roughly. Two consumers, one fixed supply.}
\telosfact{Surface area}{A block of wood is hard to light; sawdust flashes. Powdered flour explodes. The chemistry has not changed --- only how much fuel is touching oxygen at once. Hairspray is an aerosol for exactly this reason.}
\telosfact{Predict the launcher}{Your chamber is about \qty{1.4}{L}. Roughly a fifth of that is oxygen. Before you ever fire it, argue out how much fuel could possibly be burnt by that much oxygen --- and notice how small the answer is.}
\telosfact{Test it for real}{On the range, vary the spray count in half-second steps and measure range for each. You will find the peak, and you will find both failure modes either side of it.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{The link to the launcher}
Almost every complaint about a combustion launcher --- it will not fire, it
fires weakly, it fires once and then never again --- is a fuel-air ratio
problem, and almost always it is too rich rather than too lean. Once a teenager
has watched a pile of flour refuse to light while a puff of the same flour
flashes, they understand the fix without being told it.
\end{telospanel}
