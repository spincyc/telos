\telosmasthead[Crush a can with nothing]
\lessonhead{1}{about 60 minutes}{everyone --- kitchen care only}

\begin{materials}
\item Empty aluminium drink cans
\item Tongs, a hob, a bowl of iced water
\item A boiled egg, peeled, and a glass bottle it will not quite fit into
\item A drinking glass, card, water
\item A balloon and a plastic bottle
\item Bathroom scales, a bicycle pump with a gauge
\end{materials}

\begin{predict}
Put a tablespoon of water in an empty can, boil it until steam pours out, then
turn the can upside down into iced water. What happens, and \emph{how fast}?
Write both down. Then: how much does the air in the room weigh?
\end{predict}

\lessonsection{Do it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Crush the can.} A tablespoon of water in the can, on the hob until steam is streaming out for a good ten seconds. Then, with tongs, invert it into iced water. It collapses instantly and loudly. Nothing touched it.
\item \textbf{The glass of water.} Fill a glass to the brim, lay a card over it, invert. The card stays put and the water does not fall out. Air is pushing \emph{up} on it.
\item \textbf{Turn it sideways.} Rotate the inverted glass slowly. The card stays on at every angle, so the push is not gravity and it is not downward --- pressure acts in every direction at once.
\item \textbf{The egg in the bottle.} Drop a burning strip of paper into the bottle and sit the peeled egg on the neck. As the flame goes out and the gas cools, the egg is pushed in.
\item \textbf{Weigh the push.} Pump a bicycle tyre to a known pressure and read the gauge. Then work out the force on a 3\,in disc at that pressure --- the same disc as your launcher's chamber end.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonsection{What it means}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Air has mass}{Roughly \qty{1.2}{kg} per cubic metre. The air in an ordinary bedroom weighs about as much as a small child --- something like \qty{50}{kg}. It is easy to forget because you are floating in it.}
\telosfact{And therefore pressure}{The column of atmosphere above you presses at about \qty{14.7}{psi}, or \qty{101}{kPa} --- roughly \qty{10}{tonnes} on a square metre. You do not notice because it presses equally on every side of you.}
\telosfact{Nothing sucks}{The can was not sucked in. Steam drove the air out, then condensed to a few drops of water, leaving very little inside --- and the ordinary outside air pressure, no longer balanced, crushed it. There is no such thing as suction; there is only pressure on one side that is not matched on the other.}
\telosfact{This is the whole launcher}{The launcher does the same thing in reverse: it raises the pressure \emph{inside} until the imbalance drives a potato out. Both are the same idea.}
\telosfact{The number that matters}{On a 3\,in bore, one atmosphere of \emph{extra} pressure is about \qty{100}{lb} of force. That is why a modest pressure moves a heavy potato fast.}
\end{telosfacts}

\lessonsection{Break it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{More water}{Try a whole cup in the can instead of a spoonful. Does it crush harder, or not at all? Why might too much water be worse?}
\telosfact{No ice}{Invert into room-temperature water. The effect is weaker. What does that tell you about how fast the steam needs to condense?}
\telosfact{A steel can}{Try a food tin. Nothing happens. The force is the same --- so what changed?}
\telosfact{Do the arithmetic}{Area of a 3\,in circle is about \qty{7}{in^2}. At \qty{14.7}{psi} that is over \qty{100}{lb} of force from ordinary air. Now imagine three atmospheres.}
\end{telosfacts}

\end{telospanel}
