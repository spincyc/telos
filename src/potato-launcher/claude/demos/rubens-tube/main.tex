\begin{teloshazard}{Propane in a perforated pipe --- outdoors only}
This is a pipe full of flammable gas with an open flame along its length. Build
and run it \textbf{outdoors}, never indoors, never in a garage, never under
anything. An adult owns the cylinder, the regulator and the valve, and lights it
--- the same rule as the launcher's fuel. Purge and light promptly: a tube left
filling unlit accumulates gas and then lights all at once. Keep the extinguisher
on the bench, not in the house.
\end{teloshazard}

\begin{predict}
Sound is a travelling wave. Play a steady tone into a closed tube full of gas
with a row of small flames along the top. Do the flames ripple along like waves
on water, all flicker together, or something else? Draw what you expect before
you light it.
\end{predict}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.22]
\telosfact{What happens}{The flames settle into a fixed pattern of tall and short that does not move. Change the frequency and the pattern changes to a different fixed arrangement.}
\telosfact{Why}{The tone reflects off the sealed end and interferes with itself. At the right frequencies the two waves cancel at fixed points and reinforce at others, producing a \textbf{standing wave} --- a wave whose peaks stay put.}
\telosfact{Flame height reads pressure}{Higher pressure inside pushes more gas out of the holes there, so a tall flame marks an antinode and a short one a node. You are looking at a graph of pressure along the tube, drawn in fire.}
\telosfact{Measure the speed of sound}{Twice the distance between adjacent nodes is one wavelength. Multiply by the frequency and you have the speed of sound \emph{in propane} --- around \qty{250}{m/s}, noticeably slower than in air, because the gas is heavier.}
\telosfact{The link to the launcher}{Same physics, different use: pressure in a closed volume of gas, and gas that burns only where fuel and air are in the right proportion. \sheetref{2. Combustion}}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{Build it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pipe.} A 1\,m to 2\,m length of steel or copper pipe, \qtyrange{50}{75}{mm} across. \textbf{Not PVC:} this one has open flame along its whole length, and the pipe gets hot. The launcher's pressure-rated PVC rule does not transfer here --- different job, different material.
\item \textbf{Drill the holes.} A row of \qty{1.5}{mm} holes along the top, evenly spaced every \qtyrange{15}{25}{mm}, all the way down, in a straight line. Mark the line first and carry it the whole length. \sheetref{Craft 1: Measure \& Mark}
\item \textbf{Deburr every hole.} A burr changes the flame at that hole and puts a false reading in your pattern. \sheetref{Craft 3: Deburr \& Chamfer}
\item \textbf{Seal one end} with a solid cap. The reflection off this end is what makes the standing wave; a leaky end gives a poor pattern.
\item \textbf{Fit the speaker} at the other end over a flexible membrane --- a rubber sheet or a balloon stretched and clamped. It must seal the tube while still moving freely.
\item \textbf{Gas inlet} in the underside near the middle, with a valve. Adult keeps the valve.
\item \textbf{Leak-check with soapy water} before any flame goes near it, at full working pressure. Bubbles anywhere means stop.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Run it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Purge first}{Let gas flow long enough to displace the air inside, then light along the row promptly. Do not let it fill unlit.}
\telosfact{Find the resonances}{Sweep a tone generator slowly from about \qty{50}{Hz} upward. Most frequencies do nothing interesting; at particular ones the pattern snaps into place. Those are the tube's resonances --- exactly the behaviour from \sheetref{8. Resonance} in the electricity course, in a different medium.}
\telosfact{Count the nodes}{Each resonance has one more node than the last. Record frequency against node count; the relationship is a straight line and you can predict the next one before you find it.}
\telosfact{Do the arithmetic}{Node spacing times two is the wavelength. Wavelength times frequency is the speed of sound in the gas. Compare with \qty{343}{m/s} in air and explain the difference.}
\telosfact{Then play music}{Once the measurements are done. A bass line makes the whole tube dance, and it is worth every minute of the build.}
\end{telosfacts}

\begin{telospanel}{Why this is worth building}
Standing waves are on every physics syllabus and almost nobody has seen one. A
diagram of nodes and antinodes is an abstraction; a metre of fire holding
perfectly still while a speaker plays is not. Your boys will be able to point at
a node and say what it is, and they will still be able to in ten years.
\end{telospanel}
