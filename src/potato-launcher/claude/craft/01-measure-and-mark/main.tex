\begin{standard}
A mark is \textbf{one line}, thin enough that you can tell which side of it to
cut on, drawn square around the whole circumference of round stock, and made
from a measurement you took twice. If you cannot say which side of your own
mark the material comes off, the mark is not good enough.
\end{standard}

\begin{telosfacts}[0.24]
\telosfact{Measure twice}{Not a proverb --- a procedure. Take the measurement, put the tape down, do something else for ten seconds, take it again. If the two disagree, take a third. Two agreeing measurements is evidence; one is a hope.}
\telosfact{Mark thin}{A pencil line is about half a millimetre wide, which on four joints is two millimetres of accumulated slop. Use a fine mechanical pencil, or scribe with a knife where a scratch is acceptable.}
\telosfact{Mark which side}{Put a small \textbf{X} on the waste side of every line, immediately. Cut so the blade eats the X and the line survives. This single habit removes most short-piece mistakes.}
\telosfact{Go all the way round}{On pipe, a mark on one side tells you nothing about the far side. Roll the pipe against a square, or wrap paper round it, and carry the line the whole way.}
\telosfact{One reference}{Measure every cut from the \emph{same} end of the stock, not from the last cut. Measuring cut-to-cut adds every previous error to the next one.}
\telosfact{Write it down}{Cut list first, on paper, before any tool comes out. \sheetref{Build Log}}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{The wrap trick, and why it works}
Wrap a sheet of paper around the pipe so the edge meets itself exactly. Because
the paper edge is straight and the pipe is round, the line where the paper
overlaps is automatically perpendicular to the pipe axis. Draw along it.

\expectcraft{The line closes on itself when you have gone all the way round. If
the two ends of the line miss each other, the paper was not aligned and the mark
is not square --- start again rather than splitting the difference.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Seat the tape at one clean reference end. \textbf{Check:} the hook touches,
the blade lies straight, and your eye is above the graduation. If any condition
fails, reseat the tape before reading it.
\item Read the length twice from that same end, putting the tape down between
readings. \textbf{Check:} the readings agree within 1\,mm. If not, take a third
reading; use the two that agree or inspect the tape and reference end.
\item Wrap the paper until its edge meets itself, then draw one thin line along
that edge. \textbf{Check:} the paper edge has no step at the overlap. If it has
a step, erase the line and rewrap.
\item Remove the paper and inspect the line closure. \textbf{Check:} the ends
meet within 1\,mm. If not, erase the line and repeat Step 3.
\item Put one X on the waste side. \textbf{Check:} point to the kept piece and
say ``the blade removes the X.'' If that statement is wrong, move the X before
cutting.
\item Measure once more from the reference end to the surviving side of the
line. \textbf{Check:} it matches the cut list within 1\,mm. If not, erase the
mark and restart at Step 1.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{telospanel}{Pass, correct, or remake}
\textbf{Pass:} two measurements agree within 1\,mm, the line closes within
1\,mm, and the waste side is unambiguous. \textbf{Correct:} erase a faint,
wrong mark completely and repeat the whole sequence. \textbf{Remake the cut
plan:} if several dark lines cannot be distinguished, move to sound unmarked
stock; never guess which line was intended.
\end{telospanel}

\begin{telospanel}{Why this sheet is first}
Every later skill is downstream of this one. A cut cannot be squarer than its
mark; a joint cannot be tighter than its cut; a chamber cannot be stronger than
its joint. Accuracy is not a talent you have or lack --- it is a set of small
habits, and they are all on this page.
\end{telospanel}

\section{Measurement and closure record}
\noindent Use one row for each intended cut. Keep the tape seated at the same
reference end for all three readings. Measure the closure gap where the two
ends of the wrapped line meet; write \(0\) if they meet exactly.
