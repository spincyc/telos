\section{Parts}

\toprule
\textbf{Part} & \textbf{Typical} & \textbf{Notes}\\
\midrule
Chamber & 3\,in Sch 40 pressure PVC, about 12\,in long & The volume that holds the fuel and air. Pressure-rated only.\\
Cleanout & 3\,in threaded cleanout adapter and plug & The breech. Screws off to load fuel, to vent a misfire, and to wipe residue out. Do not glue the plug.\\
Reducer & 3\,in to 1.5\,in bushing or reducing coupler & Joins chamber to barrel.\\
Barrel & 1.5\,in Sch 40 pressure PVC, about 30\,in & Sized so a potato is a firm push fit.\\
Igniter & BBQ or gas-lighter piezo unit & Two electrodes into the chamber, 3--5\,mm apart, roughly a third of the way along.\\
Electrodes & Two stainless screws with nuts and washers & Through drilled and sealed holes in the chamber wall.\\
Cement & PVC primer and Sch 40 solvent cement & Both. Primer is not optional; it softens the surface so the weld actually forms.\\
Fuel & Aerosol hairspray & Cheap, widely available, and hard to over-deliver badly in a single measured burst.\\
Ammunition & Potatoes slightly larger than the bore & Firm ones. A soft potato disintegrates instead of sealing.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cut square.} A mitre box or a pipe cutter. An out-of-square cut does not seat fully and leaves a weak joint.
\item \textbf{Deburr inside and out.} A knife or sandpaper. A burr inside the barrel scores every potato and a burr inside a socket ruins the weld.
\item \textbf{Dry-fit everything.} Check the whole assembly before any cement comes out. Mark each joint with a pencil line across it so you can see if it has rotated home.
\item \textbf{Drill the electrode holes} in the chamber, about a third of the way from the breech. Fit the screws with washers and nuts and seal them with epoxy on the outside. Set the internal gap at 3--5\,mm and check that the piezo sparks across it before you glue anything.
\item \textbf{Prime, then cement.} Primer on both surfaces, cement on both surfaces, push together with a quarter-turn twist, and \textbf{hold for thirty seconds} --- solvent joints push themselves apart while they set.
\item \textbf{Write today's date on the chamber} and leave it \textbf{48 hours}. Not four. This is the step everybody shortens and it is the step that matters.
\end{enumerate}

\section{First firing}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Walk the range. Mark the firing line. Read the checklist on the \textbf{Safety} sheet aloud.
\item \textbf{Test the spark} with the cleanout open and no fuel: you should hear and see it clearly across the gap.
\item \textbf{Load the potato} from the muzzle. Push it in about 6\,in with a dowel --- firm, sealing, but not so deep that it starts against the reducer.
\item \textbf{Open the cleanout, spray a measured one-second burst}, and screw the plug fully home. The adult does this and nobody else.
\item \textbf{Shoulder or brace it, muzzle downrange}, everyone behind the line, call the shot, and fire.
\item If it does not fire: \textbf{sixty seconds}, muzzle still downrange. Then open the cleanout, fan it out, wait, and start over with a fresh dose. Never add fuel to a failed shot.
\item Between shots, \textbf{open the cleanout and fan the chamber} --- a chamber full of exhaust has no oxygen in it and the next shot will fail if you do not.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Tuning it}
\begin{telosfacts}[0.20]
\telosfact{Fuel first}{Vary the spray count in half-second steps, three shots each, and measure range. There is a clear peak, and both sides of it are worse. That peak is the stoichiometric ratio you predicted in Lesson 2.}
\telosfact{Then the potato}{Too loose and gas blows past it; too tight and it will not accelerate. A firm push fit that takes real effort with a dowel is right.}
\telosfact{Then the barrel}{If you built a spare barrel, compare. Longer gives the pressure more distance to work over, up to the point where friction and falling pressure take over. Lesson 3 predicts roughly where.}
\telosfact{Keep a log}{Date, fuel dose, barrel, angle, range. Three columns and a pencil. Without it you are guessing, and with it you have a real experiment.}
\telosfact{Air it out}{Between every shot. This one habit fixes most reliability complaints.}
\end{telosfacts}

\section{If it does not work}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.26\linewidth}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Symptom} & \textbf{Look at}\\
\midrule
No spark & Piezo faulty, gap too wide, or an electrode loose. Test with the cleanout open, always.\\
Spark but no bang & Too much fuel, far more often than too little. Vent, fan out, and try a shorter burst. Or the chamber still holds exhaust from the last shot.\\
Weak pop, potato dribbles out & Under-fuelled, or the potato is not sealing. Check the fit first.\\
Bang but poor range & Potato too loose, barrel too short, or the potato is soft and disintegrating.\\
Fires only sometimes & Residue on the electrodes, or you are not airing the chamber between shots.\\
Any crazing or whitening & Stop. Retire the chamber. \sheetref{Safety}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{telospanel}{What you built}
A single-cycle heat engine. Fuel and air burn in a fixed volume, the pressure
rises by roughly a factor of three, and that pressure does work on the base of a
potato for the length of the barrel. Everything you measured in the four lessons
--- the pressure of the atmosphere, the fuel-air ratio, the gas law, the
projectile's arc --- is happening in that fifth of a second, and you can predict
the range before you fire it.
\end{telospanel}
