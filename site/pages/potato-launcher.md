# Potato Launcher

A combustion-driven PVC launcher, built and fired on open ground, with four
lessons that make it a physics project rather than a stunt.

It is a heat engine that runs once. Fuel and air burn in a closed chamber, the
gas gets hot, hot gas pushes hard, and the only thing free to move is the
potato. Each lesson is one term in that sentence, and each is measured rather
than asserted — because before you fire it you can put a number on the pressure,
a number on the force, and a number on how far it will go.

- [Course Map](doc/potato-launcher/overview.pdf) — the through-line, who does what, and the machine

## Safety

**A potato leaving this launcher carries energy in the same range as a handgun
round** — less speed, far more mass. It will travel several hundred feet and it
will kill a person or an animal it hits. The fact that it is made of plumbing
parts changes nothing about what it does downrange.

- [**Safety**](doc/potato-launcher/safety.pdf) — the six rules, choosing ground, and the pre-shot checklist

Read it start to finish before you build, and post it where you fire. Local
ordinances vary; confirm yours before you start.

## One specification that is not negotiable

Schedule 40 **pressure**-rated PVC, marked with an NSF-PW rating and a pressure
figure. Never DWV, foam-core or cellular-core pipe — those carry no internal
pressure rating and fail by shattering rather than splitting. And never
compressed air in this design: PVC under sustained pneumatic pressure stores far
more energy and fails catastrophically.
