# Pine Lake evidence record

Checked 2026-07-26. Waterbody identity is fixed by WBIC, not by name.

## Identity and physical character

- **Water:** Pine Lake, Waukesha County, WBIC 779200.
- **DNR facts:** 711 acres; 85 ft maximum; 39 ft mean; spring lake; bottom
  recorded as 90% gravel, 5% sand, 5% muck; one boat landing; oligotrophic.
- **DNR contour source:** Wisconsin Conservation Department survey dated
  1955-06-06. The sheet reports 698 acres, 6.7 shoreline miles and 85 ft
  maximum. It is historical and marked not for navigation.
- **Current shoreline check:** 2024 Wisconsin statewide NAIP, 0.6 m aerial
  orthophotography, was consulted for shoreline, island, road and access
  context. NAIP is USDA/FSA public-domain imagery. No imagery is reproduced.

## Species evidence for publication filtering

The current DNR lake record explicitly names **panfish, largemouth bass,
smallmouth bass, northern pike and walleye**. Those groups are safe inclusions.

SEWRPC Memorandum Report 145 (2007), drawing on Wisconsin DNR records, reports
that the 1963 fishery included panfish, largemouth bass, northern pike and
walleye and records additional taxa including sunfish, blackchin shiner,
common carp and blacknose shiner. SEWRPC Memorandum Report 173 (2008) cites a
2005 DNR comprehensive survey and a 1984 cisco-kill record, and documents
1995--2006 northern-pike and walleye stocking. These older records support
historical presence, not current abundance.

**Compendium rule:** include the five current DNR groups. Cisco, common carp,
blackchin shiner and blacknose shiner may be discussed only as dated historical
records until a current inventory or survey confirms them. Do not infer
muskellunge, trout, catfish, whitefish or invasive gobies merely because the
regulation lookup contains categories for them.

## 2026--27 hook-and-line rules

DNR lookup for WBIC 779200, checked 2026-07-26:

- motor trolling: up to three hooks, baits or lures per angler;
- bass: catch-and-release year-round; harvest May 2, 2026--March 7, 2027,
  14 in minimum, five daily;
- northern pike: May 2, 2026--March 7, 2027, 26 in minimum, two daily;
- walleye/sauger/hybrids: May 2, 2026--March 7, 2027, 18 in minimum, three daily;
- panfish: open all year, no minimum, 25 daily;
- rock/yellow/white bass: open all year, no minimum, unlimited;
- cisco/whitefish: open all year, no minimum, ten daily;
- muskellunge/hybrids: May 2--December 31, 2026 on open water, 40 in minimum,
  one daily.

The lookup itself says categories may not reflect actual species. Regulations
are volatile: re-run the exact-water lookup and inspect launch signage before
every trip.

## Access and ordinances

The DNR record lists one boat landing and multiple Village of Chenequa/Town of
Merton ordinances, including slow-no-wake hours/areas and speed restrictions.
DNR warns that its ordinance database can lag and says launch signage is the
only definitive current check. The map therefore marks the DNR-recorded access
but prints no unverified fee or hour.

## Map interpretation

The line map was independently drafted from the 1955 source sheet, checked
against 2024 NAIP only for present shoreline/access context. Numbered areas are
structure-based starting hypotheses, not catch reports: north narrows; northeast
points; north-central deep edge; mid-lake island saddle; west shelf; west
pocket; southeast inside turn; southwest basin edge; south narrows/access.
Depth and shoreline are generalized and not for navigation.
