# Rig and terminal-tackle evidence record

Checked 2026-07-26. The eleven retained rigs are slip bobber, split shot,
jig-and-minnow, Lindy rig, Carolina rig, drop shot, Texas rig, wacky rig,
three-way rig, quick-strike rig and tip-up.

Wisconsin DNR's Hook, Line & Thinker curriculum supports teaching a rig as an
assembled system whose components each have a purpose. DNR's species-starting
points support bobber/live-bait presentations for panfish, jigs and live bait
for walleye, and weed/cover presentations for bass and pike. DNR release
guidance supports tackle strong enough to shorten fights and tackle choices
that reduce deep hooking. DNR ice guidance supports the tip-up as a repeatable
near-bottom live-bait presentation, but says no ice is completely safe.

No twelfth rig was added. The eleven already span float, bottom-contact,
vertical, weedless, trolling/drifting, predator live-bait and ice
presentations material to Pine and North Lakes. Adding another named assembly
would increase taxonomy without filling a clear lake-specific gap.

## Universal legal and safety bounds

- Resolve the live DNR rules for the exact water, species and date.
- Pine and North currently allow motor trolling with up to three hooks, baits
  or lures per angler; recheck before departure.
- Wisconsin bait, live-fish transport and aquatic-invasive-species rules
  control what may be carried and moved.
- Quick-strike rigs require prompt attention and equipment capable of landing
  the target rapidly; never leave live-bait gear unattended.
- Tip-ups do not make ice safe. Avoid current, travel with others, carry
  flotation, picks, rope and a waterproofed phone, and obtain current local
  information.
- Crimping barbs where practical, using non-stainless hooks when a hook may be
  left, and cutting line rather than excavating a deep hook can reduce injury.
