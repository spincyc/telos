# Electricity & Magnetism — atomic shared claims

Each claim is deliberately small enough to accept, reject, or revise
independently. “Support” identifiers resolve in [sources.md](sources.md).

| ID | Atomic reusable claim | Support | Notes / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-SAFE-001 | A shock occurs when a body becomes part of an electrical current path. | S02 | Avoid “voltage jumps into you” language. |
| E-SAFE-002 | Shock outcome depends on current magnitude, path, duration, and frequency. | S02 | Do not reduce safety to a single current number. |
| E-SAFE-003 | Live parts should ordinarily be deenergized before exposed work. | S01 | Workplace rule; sound hierarchy-of-controls principle. |
| E-SAFE-004 | A switch position or indicator alone does not prove an exposed circuit is deenergized; appropriate test equipment is required in the OSHA procedure. | S01 | Only a qualified person performs OSHA verification. |
| E-SAFE-005 | OSHA restricts energized exposed-parts work to qualified persons. | S01 | “Adult” and “qualified” are not synonyms. |
| E-SAFE-006 | OSHA treats 50 V as a widely recognized hazardous-shock threshold. | S03 | It is not a universal safe/unsafe dividing line. |
| E-SAFE-007 | Below 50 V, burns, arcs, fire, stored energy, source current, and environmental conditions can still create hazards. | S01, S03 | Inference from the conditional sub-50 V exception. |
| E-SAFE-008 | Damaged cords, plugs and receptacles are shock/fire hazards and belong outside learner experiments. | S08, S09 | Inspection is observation only; no learner repair. |
| E-SAFE-009 | Telos learner investigations may use only isolated, current-limited battery sources at 12 V DC or less. | S01–S03 | Conservative editorial control, not a quoted statutory threshold. |
| E-SAFE-010 | Learners do not open, modify, probe, or construct equipment connected to household power. | S01, S02, S08 | Telos editorial rule. |
| E-SAFE-011 | Large batteries and charged capacitors require separate risk assessment because low voltage does not limit available fault energy by itself. | S01, S03 | Engineering inference; source current and stored energy matter. |
| E-PHY-001 | Current is rate of charge flow; one ampere is one coulomb per second. | S04 | SI definition relationship. |
| E-PHY-002 | One volt equals one watt per ampere, and one ohm equals one volt per ampere. | S04 | Supports dimensional reasoning. |
| E-PHY-003 | The elementary charge magnitude is exactly \(1.602176634\times10^{-19}\) C in SI. | S05 | Fixed defining constant. |
| E-PHY-004 | A sustained current in a simple circuit requires a closed conducting path and a source of potential difference. | S07 | Conventional-current model is adequate at this level. |
| E-PHY-005 | For an ohmic element under stable conditions, voltage, current and resistance obey \(V=IR\). | S06, S07 | State the stable-condition limitation. |
| E-PHY-006 | Electrical power transferred to a component is \(P=VI\); electrical energy over an interval is power multiplied by time. | S06 | Avoid claiming all input becomes useful output. |
| E-PHY-007 | Electric charge comes in two signs; like signs repel and unlike signs attract. | S06 | Macroscopic introductory model. |
| E-PHY-008 | Current in a conductor produces a magnetic field around it. | S06 | Field direction is conventional-current direction. |
| E-PHY-009 | A changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an emf; induced effects oppose the change that produces them. | S06 | Faraday–Lenz qualitative form. |
| E-PHY-010 | Motors and generators are reciprocal energy-conversion contexts: current in magnetic fields can produce motion, while changing flux can produce electrical output. | S06 | Neither process creates energy. |
| E-PHY-011 | A voltmeter is connected across two points; an ammeter is placed in the current path. | S06 | Meter rating and lead-jack checks still required. |
| E-PED-001 | Prediction, controlled change, measurement, graphing and explanation distinguish an investigation from a demonstration. | Editorial synthesis | Pedagogical choice, not a sourced physical fact. |
| E-PED-002 | Circuit diagrams should express electrical connections rather than imitate physical layout. | Editorial synthesis | Supports transfer across different breadboards. |
