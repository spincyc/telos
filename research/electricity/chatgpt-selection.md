# ChatGPT edition — source and claim selection

**Edition:** ChatGPT
**Selected:** 2026-07-26
**Scope:** Printable Electricity & Magnetism investigations for a learner and
an adult facilitator.

## Salience decisions

1. **The boundary precedes the physics.** E-SAFE-001–011 govern every sheet.
   The course uses isolated battery supplies at 12 V DC or less, but never calls
   that boundary “safe voltage.” Current capability, shorts, heat, damaged
   cells and stored energy still matter.
2. **Adult ownership is not electrical qualification.** E-SAFE-005 is the most
   important distinction missing from many hobby treatments. The adult owns
   materials and stop decisions. Mains/high-voltage work is absent; if such
   work ever occurs elsewhere, it belongs to an appropriately qualified person.
3. **Measurement is the spine.** E-PHY-001–006 and E-PHY-011 turn vocabulary
   into falsifiable circuit work. Every investigation asks for a prediction, a
   changed variable, recorded units and a claim supported by measurements.
4. **Fields are inferred from effects.** E-PHY-007–010 are organized as three
   related maps—electric interaction, magnetic effect of current, and induction—
   rather than as a march toward a high-voltage machine.
5. **Energy bookkeeping prevents magic-language.** E-PHY-006 and E-PHY-010 are
   selected so motors and generators are described as conversions, not creation.

## Source choices

- **Primary safety spine:** S01–S03 and S08–S09. OSHA supplies the work-control
  logic; CPSC keeps the home context visible.
- **Measurement spine:** S04–S05. NIST anchors units and charge without relying
  on an unsourced textbook summary.
- **Stable physics relationships:** S06–S07. DOE material is authoritative and
  broad, though S06 is archived; only stable introductory relationships are used.

## Claims intentionally not selected

- Numerical “let-go” or fatal-current tables: outcomes vary with path, time,
  frequency and contact; a compact threshold can invite false precision.
- A claim that “under 50 V is safe”: contradicted by OSHA’s conditional language
  and by stored/fault-energy hazards.
- DIY household wiring, microwave-transformer, Tesla-coil, spark-gap, mains
  transformer, or large-capacitor procedures: outside the learner domain.
- The water-pressure analogy as a central model: occasionally helpful but it
  obscures charge conservation and can be mistaken for mechanism.
